## 1. Plucking: direction, finger and hand motion, force, speed, double and triple strokes, re-plucking a ringing lamella

### Takeaway
Teachers describe three families of stroke: a single-direction pluck (toward or away from the player), a two-direction "there and back" stroke made by a loose wrist or forearm for speed, and a multi-finger roll (index, middle, ring, sometimes little finger in turn) for tremolo and ornaments. The only speed figure found is Wayland Harman's "as many as 1,000 times per minute", in short bursts; every teacher puts evenness of timing and loudness before speed, and names the lamella clanking on the frame as the fault of a crooked or uneven pluck.

### Cited Findings

**Direction of a single pluck (sources disagree on the default)**
- `[DESCRIBED]` Wayland Harman divides plucks into "innies" and "outies". "The OUT pluck pushes the reed away from the player before it snaps back, free to vibrate until the next pluck." It is made with the index finger "sideways across the tip, moving in a somewhat circular movement, bending at the elbow or wrist or both", or with the thumb. "The IN pluck pulls the reed toward the player before release", most basically "with a finger tip using the finger muscle much like a concert harpist plucks a string", the hand still and the thumb resting on the cheek. — [Harman, Mouthing Off #4: Advanced Playing Techniques (Jew's Harp Guild)](http://www.jewsharpguild.org/whmo4.html)
- `[DESCRIBED]` Harman on the commonest method: "use the index finger to push the reed out and away. The fingers are held perpendicular to the reed and powered by wrist action. This method can be expanded to create a rapid out/in plucking action - good for fast rhythms". His own preference: elbow raised, fingers parallel to the lamella, plucking with the middle finger "using only the finger muscles". — [Harman, Mouthing Off #2: How to play a steel Jew's harp](http://www.jewsharpguild.org/whmo2.html)
- `[DESCRIBED]` Nizhny Novgorod vargan school (text by Oleg Aronov): one-finger playing is "a strike or pluck of the index finger on the bent tip of the lamella in the direction 'toward oneself'. This is the basic and simplest technique of finger technique, traditional for folk performers." The strike is made with the pad or the side of the index finger. — [Aronov, "Exercises with the vargan. Nizhny Novgorod vargan school", vargan.ru (archived copy read; the live site no longer resolves)](http://web.archive.org/web/20200225231341/http://www.vargan.ru:80/varganzal/varganschools/94-uprazhneniya-s-varganom-nizhegorodskaya-vargannaya-shkola)
- `[DESCRIBED]` Spiridon Shishigin (Sakha khomus): the *main stroke* is "a touch by the second phalanx of the bent index finger while the wrist rotates toward oneself about the vertical axis"; the *reverse stroke* touches the lamella on the return rotation; the *alternating stroke* ("переменный удар") touches it going both ways and "is used when playing at a fast tempo; various combinations of main and reverse stroke can be used". A fourth type, the *side finger stroke* ("боковой пальцевый удар"): the hand does not move and is turned to the face, the fingers set the lamella moving "with plucking strikes", the thumb may rest on the face between eye and ear. — [Interview with S. S. Shishigin by V. Markov, varganist.ru](https://varganist.ru/53.html)
- `[DESCRIBED]` Robert Vandré: one outstretched finger plucks the lamella "from below, away from the mouth"; it can also pluck the bent end "toward the mouth"; "plucked back and forth, fast note sequences can be played" (his sound example 1). His beginner instruction is to move the tip "a little away from the mouth and then let it slip". — [Vandré, Spieltechnik (rvandre.de)](http://www.rvandre.de/spieltechnik.html); [Vandré, Einstieg für Anfänger](http://www.rvandre.de/einstieg.html)
- `[DESCRIBED]` Jew's Harp Guild beginner sheet: "With your forefinger, pluck the tongue of the Jew's Harp, either by pushing or pulling." — [Jew's Harp Guild, How to Play the Jew's Harp (updated June 2011)](http://www.jewsharpguild.org/play.html)
- `[CLAIMED]` French Wikipedia: the other hand works the lamella "most often from the outside toward the inside"; "some players make the lamella vibrate with one finger, others with several, others with their hand. The action on the lamella determines the rhythm." — [fr.wikipedia: Guimbarde (instrument)](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guimbarde_(instrument))
- `[CLAIMED]` Hungarian Wikipedia: the free end is plucked "with the index finger or the thumb". Czech Wikipedia: plucked with the right index finger; "plucking is possible in both directions, especially for quick changes of note and tempo". — [hu.wikipedia: Doromb](https://hu.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doromb); [cs.wikipedia: Brumle](https://cs.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brumle)
- `[CLAIMED]` Marin Mersenne (1636), as quoted by English Wikipedia: "one may strike the tongue with the index finger in two ways, i.e., by lifting it or lowering it". — [en.wikipedia: Jew's harp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jew%27s_harp)

**Two-direction (double) strokes and wrist / forearm motion**
- `[DESCRIBED]` Aronov's classification: strokes are *one-sided*, *two-sided* or *combined*. One-sided strokes are grouped by count: single, paired, triple, quadruple, and "variant" (mixed, e.g. paired-triple, paired-single-quadruple). Two-sided strokes move "toward oneself - away from oneself" and are grouped "in pairs (toward - away) or in threes (toward - away - toward, away - toward - away)". The technique is called "vibrational"; it "is possible only with a relaxed right wrist and is therefore called 'wrist playing'". — [Aronov, Nizhny Novgorod vargan school](http://web.archive.org/web/20200225231341/http://www.vargan.ru:80/varganzal/varganschools/94-uprazhneniya-s-varganom-nizhegorodskaya-vargannaya-shkola)
- `[DESCRIBED]` Aronov, *wrist stroke from below*: palm flat, fingers pointing up, motion only in the wrist joint, the pad of the striking finger ("as a rule the middle finger") touching lightly. "The sound must be even both on strokes away and on strokes toward oneself; it must not 'jump' or die away, and there must be no clank of the lamella against the frame arms" ("не должно быть также лязга язычка о деки"). Exercise 4 is the even two-sided stroke "with a gradual rise and fall of loudness". — [Aronov, Nizhny Novgorod vargan school](http://web.archive.org/web/20200225231341/http://www.vargan.ru:80/varganzal/varganschools/94-uprazhneniya-s-varganom-nizhegorodskaya-vargannaya-shkola)
- `[DESCRIBED]` Aronov, *wrist stroke from above, Yakut style*: index finger pointing down, wrist bent with its inner side to the face, elbow at or a little above shoulder height and motionless; the strike is made with the side of the second phalanx of the index finger, "ALONG A TANGENT"; the muscles tense "only at the moment of the strike, a very short interval". It "combines power and speed" and suits both very slow overtone-rich tunes and very fast "juicy" pieces with more low frequencies. — [Aronov, Nizhny Novgorod vargan school](http://web.archive.org/web/20200225231341/http://www.vargan.ru:80/varganzal/varganschools/94-uprazhneniya-s-varganom-nizhegorodskaya-vargannaya-shkola)
- `[DESCRIBED]` Harman: "In plucking both directions, a half moon back and forth motion is often used. This can be wrist rotation or just one digit. A more straight line approach can be had from the elbow and will usually allow more rapid plucks." — [Harman, Mouthing Off #4](http://www.jewsharpguild.org/whmo4.html)
- `[DESCRIBED]` Forum player on fast playing: the two-sided stroke is the most efficient "because the hand does not make one wasted movement for each useful one"; the whole forearm gives the impulse and the hand and finger "fly after it by inertia"; at speed the forearm travel is "up to 1 cm" ("до 1 см"); practise slowly along a wide path "from the ear to a point 20 cm in front of the nose" ("в 20 см перед носом"), which shrinks "to a couple of centimetres"; the finger should graze the ring of the lamella with the middle of the first phalanx "with the same force in each direction"; use a metronome. — [oberton.pro forum, "Learning the fast stroke" (2014)](https://www.oberton.pro/forum/topic/2721-%D0%BD%D0%B0%D1%83%D1%87%D0%B8%D1%82%D1%8C%D1%81%D1%8F-%D0%B1%D1%8B%D1%81%D1%82%D1%80%D0%BE%D0%BC%D1%83-%D0%B1%D0%BE%D1%8E/)
- `[CLAIMED]` Morsing (collector's note by Randy Raine-Reusch, 2002): "an instrument of virtuosity used to play very rapid complex rhythmic patterns. To achieve these rapid passage the lamella is plucked in both directions, which is why the stem is curved into a tight circle." — [ASZA: Morsing - Morchang](https://asza.com/Instruments/imorsing.shtml)
- `[DESCRIBED]` Instrument dependence: "Soft reeds are more influenced by voice and air, while stiff reeds are easier to double pluck." A full loop at the tip "keeps the finger from getting stuck as it plucks, especially when plucking both in and out". — [Harman, Mouthing Off #4](http://www.jewsharpguild.org/whmo4.html); [Harman, Mouthing Off #5 (Jew's Harp Guild)](http://www.jewsharpguild.org/whmo5.html)
- `[DESCRIBED]` Forum players: on a doromb the away-stroke scrapes the knuckle because the tip is a hook, not a ring; a stiff Yakut khomus is awkward to strike away; comfort depends "on the flexibility of the knee of the lamella, its angle and the ratio of ring diameter to finger diameter"; on a long soft lamella "fast rhythms are not great. The decay is long." — [oberton.pro forum, "Learning the fast stroke"](https://www.oberton.pro/forum/topic/2721-%D0%BD%D0%B0%D1%83%D1%87%D0%B8%D1%82%D1%8C%D1%81%D1%8F-%D0%B1%D1%8B%D1%81%D1%82%D1%80%D0%BE%D0%BC%D1%83-%D0%B1%D0%BE%D1%8E/)
- `[DESCRIBED]` Shishigin: "a khomus with a low sound rings longer and suits more moderate playing, while for playing at a fast tempo a khomus with a high tone suits better." — [Shishigin interview, varganist.ru](https://varganist.ru/53.html)

**Multi-finger rolls, tremolo, triple plucks**
- `[DESCRIBED]` Vandré, *Tremolo*: with the palm turned to the mouth, "ring finger, middle finger and index finger can pluck the lamella alternately in quick succession, as a flamenco player plucks a guitar string with three fingers"; the thumb braces the hand on the cheekbone. "This technique needs a lot of practice until the strokes come evenly and the lamella is not hit crookedly and strikes the frame." — [Vandré, Spieltechnik](http://www.rvandre.de/spieltechnik.html)
- `[DESCRIBED]` Aronov, *multi-finger technique*: alternate strikes of index, middle, ring and little finger, all "toward oneself", thumb braced on the temple. Written with finger numbers (index 1, middle 2, ring 3, little 4); fingerings such as "1-2-3-4; 2-3-3-1; 1-2-2-3; 3-4-2-3". It "lets one speed up the tempo" and play "complex, whimsical rhythmic figures [and] ornaments". — [Aronov, Nizhny Novgorod vargan school](http://web.archive.org/web/20200225231341/http://www.vargan.ru:80/varganzal/varganschools/94-uprazhneniya-s-varganom-nizhegorodskaya-vargannaya-shkola)
- `[DESCRIBED]` Harman: with the thumb resting near the eye, "multiple fingers [can] pluck in succession, a nice ability on songs with groups of short notes at key rhythmic junctures"; "interspersing double plucking with a single plucked rhythm allows for wide rhythmic possibilities". — [Harman, Mouthing Off #4](http://www.jewsharpguild.org/whmo4.html); [Harman, Mouthing Off #7: Trump Techniques](http://www.jewsharpguild.org/whmo7.html)
- `[DESCRIBED]` Sakha "horse" imitation (Varvara Stepanova's lesson, written up by V. Markov): the lamella is damped with the left hand, the first finger of the right hand rests on the cheek near the lips, and "strikes should be done on the reed by three fingers of the right hand by turns". — [Markov, "Varvara Stepanova's master class", varganist.ru](https://varganist.ru/43e.html)
- `[DESCRIBED]` A forum player lists the techniques of Vladimir Konchev's Altai school book as "trills, tremolo (with three different grips), glissando, staccato, rolls [дроби]". — [oberton.pro forum, "Which techniques do you use when playing?"](https://www.oberton.pro/forum/topic/540-%D0%BA%D0%B0%D0%BA%D0%B8%D0%B5-%D1%82%D0%B5%D1%85%D0%BD%D0%B8%D0%BA%D0%B8-%D0%B2%D1%8B-%D0%B8%D1%81%D0%BF%D0%BE%D0%BB%D1%8C%D0%B7%D1%83%D0%B5%D1%82%D0%B5-%D0%BF%D1%80%D0%B8-%D0%B8%D0%B3%D1%80%D0%B5/)

**Speed**
- `[DESCRIBED]` "Accomplished players may pluck the reed as many as 1,000 times per minute, though usually for short periods, interspersed throughout a longer piece." (A teacher's estimate, not a measurement.) — [Harman, Mouthing Off #4](http://www.jewsharpguild.org/whmo4.html)
- `[DESCRIBED]` Forum beginner (one month of playing): "the hardest stroke for me so far is tempo 50 in 4/4; tempo 120 in 4/4 comes out better" ("темп 50 4/4, темп 120 4/4"). Teachers in the same thread: evenness "is an order of magnitude more important than speed". — [oberton.pro forum, "Learning the fast stroke"](https://www.oberton.pro/forum/topic/2721-%D0%BD%D0%B0%D1%83%D1%87%D0%B8%D1%82%D1%8C%D1%81%D1%8F-%D0%B1%D1%8B%D1%81%D1%82%D1%80%D0%BE%D0%BC%D1%83-%D0%B1%D0%BE%D1%8E/)
- `[OBSERVED]` Balinese genggong (pulled by a string): "The genggong lamella has a relatively short sustain, and must be constantly kept in motion by pulling the attached string. A loose but precise snapping of the wrist at regular short intervals" keeps it sounding; interlocking parts "generally consist of sixteenth notes", while two players in their eighties played "mostly ... eighth notes", "at half the density". No tempo is given. — [Morgan 2008, "Organs and Bodies: the Jew's harp and the anthropology of musical instruments", MA thesis, UBC, ch. 5.2](https://facultystaff.richmond.edu/~amcgraw/gamelan/ubc_2008_fall_morgan_deirdre-libre.pdf)

**How hard, where, and the timbre of the pluck**
- `[DESCRIBED]` "Start softly and pluck straight. Many beginners want to play louder and feel that plucking harder is the thing to do. Usually, this is not the case. Creating accurate mouth cavity sizes and efficient use of air flow over the reed will have a much greater effect". — [Harman, Mouthing Off #2](http://www.jewsharpguild.org/whmo2.html)
- `[DESCRIBED]` Pluck geometry changes brightness: a pluck that pushes or pulls the end "perpendicular to the length of the reed" has "a slightly less brilliant tone than a pluck which grabs the tip of the trigger and pulls it away from the crimp (parallel to the reed's length)", because then "the reed is flexing toward the middle which excites the higher harmonics more"; the difference "may be quite subtle". Some players "scratch" at the tip with one or several fingers for that brightness. — [Harman, Mouthing Off #7](http://www.jewsharpguild.org/whmo7.html)
- `[DESCRIBED]` Clank against the frame: "If you pluck at an angle, the reed will deflect sideways and hit the frame. If your reed doesn't hit the frame, no matter how badly you pluck it, then you could probably close the gap a little." Holding the harp by its outer edges "may compress the playing area and cause the reed to hit the frame". — [Harman, Mouthing Off #2](http://www.jewsharpguild.org/whmo2.html)
- `[DESCRIBED]` A forum owner of a long vargan: "from time to time the lamella gives a crackle, it catches the frame arms"; she asks whether this is normal for long instruments or a matter of technique (the thread has no answer). — [oberton.pro forum, "Rattling"](https://www.oberton.pro/forum/topic/5125-%D0%B4%D1%80%D0%B5%D0%B1%D0%B5%D0%B7%D0%B6%D0%B0%D0%BD%D0%B8%D0%B5/)
- `[DESCRIBED]` Aronov: the patch of skin that strikes "differs depending on the force, speed and variant of the stroke". — [Aronov, Nizhny Novgorod vargan school](http://web.archive.org/web/20200225231341/http://www.vargan.ru:80/varganzal/varganschools/94-uprazhneniya-s-varganom-nizhegorodskaya-vargannaya-shkola)

**Touching a lamella that is already vibrating (without a normal pluck)**
- `[DESCRIBED]` Two finger techniques that Vandré attributes to Vladiswar Nadishana. *Vibrato*: pluck toward the mouth with an outstretched finger and, out of the same motion, tap the lips several times; "with each tap both the loudness and the pitch are slightly changed". *Pralltriller*: the ring finger of the holding hand briefly and lightly touches the just-plucked, vibrating lamella with the fingernail; "the fundamental is briefly raised". — [Vandré, Spieltechnik](http://www.rvandre.de/spieltechnik.html)
- `[DESCRIBED]` "Whine like a horse": "place your plucking finger on the trigger and push the reed forward and backward while blowing like crazy. Do not release the reed, just go in and out with it. Trumps with tight tolerances work best." — [Harman, Mouthing Off #7](http://www.jewsharpguild.org/whmo7.html)

### Inferences
- (Mine.) 1,000 plucks per minute is about 16.7 plucks per second, one every 60 ms. If this is done as a two-direction stroke, the hand makes about 8 full cycles per second. Sixteenth notes at 120 bpm are 8 per second (125 ms apart), so Harman's figure corresponds to sixteenths at roughly 250 bpm, which fits his "short periods" qualifier.
- (Mine.) No source describes what is *heard* when a still-ringing lamella is re-plucked. Harman's wording ("snaps back, free to vibrate until the next pluck") and Aronov's list of faults (level "jumping", "dying away", "clank") imply that each new pluck catches the moving lamella with the finger, so the previous ring is cut or disturbed for the contact time and the new level depends on the finger's force, not on the remaining amplitude. Whether a click is heard at the contact needs the ear or a recording analysis.
- (Mine.) The two pluck directions give opposite initial displacement and, by Harman's remark, possibly slightly different brightness; Aronov's demand that both directions sound equal suggests that players treat the difference as a fault to be trained away, not as an articulation. An engine could offer a small, optional up/down-stroke difference.
- (Mine.) The frame clank is tied by the sources to crooked or uneven plucks and to loud multi-finger playing, so it belongs at the hard end of the pluck-strength range and in fast rolls, as an occasional event, not as a fixed part of every loud note.
- (Mine.) Aronov's sentence that fast two-sided strokes "speed up the frequency of the lamella's vibration" cannot be literally true of the lamella's own frequency; I read it as the stroke rate becoming an audible flutter. Treat as DESCRIBED wording only.

### Gaps
- No published measurement of plucking rates (strokes per second, bpm) was found for any tradition; the only figure is Harman's estimate. Morsing concert tempi, khomus competition pieces and the like would have to be measured from recordings.
- No source says what a re-pluck of a ringing lamella sounds like (click, level jump, gap). Needs listening or waveform analysis.
- "Ghost notes" as a named jaw harp term were not found. The nearest documented thing is the accented / unaccented ("passing note") alternation of genggong playing (section 6) and damped strokes (section 2).
- Aksenty Beskrovny's article on fast plucking (axenty.livejournal.com/115092.html, recommended in the forum thread) could not be read: LiveJournal showed a login wall and the archive copy was rate-limited. It is said to analyse one grip in detail and may contain numbers.
- The morsing's stroke technique is documented here only by Wikipedia and a collector's note; no Carnatic teaching text on morsing strokes was obtained.

## 2. Damping and stopping: finger, tongue, lips, breath, throat; staccato versus let ring; muted strokes

### Takeaway
The lamella is stopped or shortened in four documented ways: a finger of the holding hand laid on or pinching the lamella (which also raises the pitch and can give a "distortion"), the tongue pushed against it ("slap tongue", "muted pop"), the lips (playing on pursed lips, or closing them on the lamella), and the breath/throat (a sudden stop "with the help of an epiglottis"). "Staccato" means three different things in the sources: a physically damped lamella, a ringing lamella chopped by tongue syllables, and (Sakha *tabygyr*) the imitation of hooves and dripping water.

### Cited Findings

**Finger on the lamella**
- `[DESCRIBED]` *Finger capo*: thumb and ring or index finger of the holding hand "clamp the lamella from both sides. The fundamental is thereby damped, but also becomes higher" (sound example 5). — [Vandré, Spieltechnik](http://www.rvandre.de/spieltechnik.html)
- `[DESCRIBED]` "Another muting technique is to place a finger on the hand holding the harp against the reed. Variations in pressure and placement greatly affect the sound. This is similar to the variety of harmonics produced by any single muted guitar string, depending upon which node is used." And: muting "can shift its pitch upward or can highlight a specific overtone ... The effect, however, is not clear like the guitar's harmonics." — [Harman, Mouthing Off #4](http://www.jewsharpguild.org/whmo4.html); [Harman, Mouthing Off #7](http://www.jewsharpguild.org/whmo7.html)
- `[DESCRIBED]` Sakha "Drops" (rain-drop imitation): "press the reed of the khomus by the index finger of your left hand ... exactly in the middle of its length from the back side of the khomus. The right hand should strike the reed from both sides", then add "side tones by breath and by movements of your tongue". — [Markov, "Varvara Stepanova's master class"](https://varganist.ru/43e.html)
- `[DESCRIBED]` A forum player: "squeezing the lamella at its centre with the fingers of the holding hand. Gives an effective distortion"; and squeezing the frame arms as far as is safe makes the sound "as if the dust had been wiped off". Shishigin, asked the same, does not squeeze the frame: "one can accidentally hinder the lamella's vibration." — [oberton.pro forum, "Which techniques do you use when playing?"](https://www.oberton.pro/forum/topic/540-%D0%BA%D0%B0%D0%BA%D0%B8%D0%B5-%D1%82%D0%B5%D1%85%D0%BD%D0%B8%D0%BA%D0%B8-%D0%B2%D1%8B-%D0%B8%D1%81%D0%BF%D0%BE%D0%BB%D1%8C%D0%B7%D1%83%D0%B5%D1%82%D0%B5-%D0%BF%D1%80%D0%B8-%D0%B8%D0%B3%D1%80%D0%B5/); [Shishigin interview](https://varganist.ru/53.html)
- `[DESCRIBED]` Markov's notation has a sign for it: "* - The reed of the jew's harp pinched by finger." — [Markov, Jew's Harp notation system, varganist.ru](https://varganist.ru/491e.html)
- `[CLAIMED]` (snippet) An online course advertises content running from "tremolo and finger damping to overtone shaping and vibrato". Course marketing copy; the lessons themselves were not seen. — [Udemy: The Jaw Harp Journey](https://www.udemy.com/course/the-jaw-harp-journey/)

**Tongue against the lamella**
- `[DESCRIBED]` *Slap tongue*: "Place the tongue's tip behind the lower front teeth and push the top of your tongue forward to the upper front teeth. Time the pluck and tongue push to create a muted pop." — [Harman, Mouthing Off #7](http://www.jewsharpguild.org/whmo7.html)
- `[DESCRIBED]` "Pushing the tongue into the reed produces a muted twang that can serve as a very nice accent. Be careful not to let the tip get between reed and frame by using the flat part of your tongue just behind the tip." — [Harman, Mouthing Off #4](http://www.jewsharpguild.org/whmo4.html)
- `[DESCRIBED]` Sakha "reed pause" (credited as a new method invented by Fedora Gogoleva): made "by a tongue, by lips or by a finger"; "slowing down is made only at inhalation"; with the tongue, turn the tip down and stop the lamella with the pad so formed; "The sequence of actions in any method is always the following one: a strike, then inhalation, then a pause, then nose-blow." With the finger, the stopping point is again "the middle of its length". — [Markov, "Varvara Stepanova's master class"](https://varganist.ru/43e.html)
- `[CLAIMED]` Classic FM, relaying morsing player Pirashanna Thevarajah: "you also have to watch out for your tongue ... you can even cut the tongue on the vibrating steel rod if you're not careful". — [Classic FM: What is a 'morsing'?](https://www.classicfm.com/discover-music/instruments/morsing-introduction-tuning-use/)

**Lips**
- `[DESCRIBED]` Aronov, *staccato*: "performed with separate short sounds. The vargan is pressed to the lips or, if the instrument is pressed to the teeth, the lamella is pressed by a finger of the holding hand. The sound is dry, abrupt, through the damping of the lamella's vibration by lips or finger. With 'staccato' one usually imitates the sound of rain drops, the clatter of hooves and so on", with varied rhythmic figures and "accents shifted to the weak beats (syncopation)". Exercises: even strokes with the vargan against the lips, "noting the dull, dry character of the sound"; the same while clicking the tongue for drops; a triple-stroke tremolo against the lips while raising and lowering the tongue. — [Aronov, Nizhny Novgorod vargan school](http://web.archive.org/web/20200225231341/http://www.vargan.ru:80/varganzal/varganschools/94-uprazhneniya-s-varganom-nizhegorodskaya-vargannaya-shkola)
- `[DESCRIBED]` Vandré, percussive effect: hold the instrument to lips pursed as for a kiss; "the sound becomes quieter and the lamella vibrates for a shorter time. With the mouth closed the plucked lamella strikes the lips. Opening the lips right after the impact produces the noises of sound example 11". — [Vandré, Spieltechnik](http://www.rvandre.de/spieltechnik.html)
- `[DESCRIBED]` "Playing a steel harp like a bamboo instrument (touching lips only) brings out a muted tone with little resonance ... I prefer a fast plucking tempo to offset the shorter sustain". — [Harman, Mouthing Off #7](http://www.jewsharpguild.org/whmo7.html)

**Breath and throat as a stop**
- `[DESCRIBED]` Markov's notation distinguishes "ha - Abrupt beginning of the sound with the help an epiglottis" and "ah - A sudden stop of a sound with the help of an epiglottis". — [Markov, Jew's Harp notation system](https://varganist.ru/491e.html)
- `[DESCRIBED]` Forum players list "smooth and sharp (up to a full stop) inhales/exhales" and, as a technique heard from morchang players, "a sharp short inhale with closing of the larynx". — [oberton.pro forum, "Which techniques do you use when playing?"](https://www.oberton.pro/forum/topic/540-%D0%BA%D0%B0%D0%BA%D0%B8%D0%B5-%D1%82%D0%B5%D1%85%D0%BD%D0%B8%D0%BA%D0%B8-%D0%B2%D1%8B-%D0%B8%D1%81%D0%BF%D0%BE%D0%BB%D1%8C%D0%B7%D1%83%D0%B5%D1%82%D0%B5-%D0%BF%D1%80%D0%B8-%D0%B8%D0%B3%D1%80%D0%B5/)

**Staccato without touching the lamella; let ring**
- `[DESCRIBED]` Vandré's *Staccato*: fast index-finger plucking combined with tongue articulation "as if speaking Düt Düt Düdldüt" gives "quickly chopped notes" (sound example 8). — [Vandré, Spieltechnik](http://www.rvandre.de/spieltechnik.html)
- `[OBSERVED]` In the Sakha tradition *tabygyr* is glossed "(staccato)" and is "associated with the sound of the horse trot and droplets of melting icicles". — [Nikolsky, Alekseyev, Alekseev & Dyakonova 2020, Frontiers in Psychology 10:3051, main text and footnote 15](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.03051/full)
- `[OBSERVED]` The morsing "is made of very thick iron, and the lamella has very high tension and short sustain. The result is a loud, high-pitched staccato effect". — [Morgan 2008, pp. 29-30](https://facultystaff.richmond.edu/~amcgraw/gamelan/ubc_2008_fall_morgan_deirdre-libre.pdf)
- `[DESCRIBED]` Let ring: "After one strike, take a long breath. A vibrating note can be sustained for as long as the inhalation or exhalation lasts." — [oberton.pro, Basics of Jaw Harp Playing](https://www.oberton.pro/en/info/play.htm)
- `[CLAIMED]` French Wikipedia (citing Leipp 1963) describes the bare lamella as vibrating "with a rather rapid decay, which can be 20 decibels per second" ("20 décibels par seconde"). — [fr.wikipedia: Guimbarde (instrument)](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guimbarde_(instrument))

### Inferences
- (Mine.) Finger and lip damping change the note from its first instant (duller, quieter, shorter, pitch raised by the finger), so they are properties of the hit: a "muted" pluck type. The tongue push and the "reed pause" are stops applied some time after the pluck, so they are an end-of-note gesture inside one pluck.
- (Mine.) The same word "staccato" covers (a) a damped lamella, (b) an undamped lamella whose mouth resonance is gated by the tongue, and (c) a genre-like imitation. For an engine these are different mechanisms: (a) shortens the source, (b) only shortens the audible resonance while the drone carries on.
- (Mine.) The 20 dB/s figure, if right, means an undamped ring of several seconds; with fast plucking almost every note is therefore cut by the next pluck, not by its own decay. The sibling notes on measurements should be preferred for the actual decay value.

### Gaps
- No source gives the duration of a damped stroke, or how fast a finger/tongue stop silences the lamella (ms).
- Whether a tongue or lip stop is silent or makes a click/buzz while the lamella rattles against soft tissue is described only by Vandré ("the noises of sound example 11"); needs the ear.
- Morsing damping practice (short sustain is attributed to the instrument, not to a damping technique) was not found in a teaching source.

## 3. Mouth gestures inside one pluck: vowel sweeps, tongue taps and trills, lips, cheeks, pharynx, named imitations

### Takeaway
The sources agree that most of the "words" of the instrument are made while one pluck rings: silent vowels and diphthongs select overtones, tongue-tip taps and consonant syllables (de-de, dü-dl, taka-taka, T/K/R) chop or trill the resonance, lip and pharynx movements add slower swells, and the Sakha tradition names fixed imitations (lark, goose, cuckoo, horse, drops) each with its own recipe. The sources name gestures by syllables, not by time: no sweep or trill rate is given anywhere.

### Cited Findings

**The principle: several articulations on one stroke**
- `[OBSERVED]` Sakha *syyia tardy* ("talking khomus") is a technique "where silent mouth articulations as though speak out syllables while the plucked lamella keeps vibrating"; it "is also used in other styles that require increased clarity of articulation". — [Nikolsky et al. 2020, footnote 16](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.03051/full)
- `[OBSERVED]` A student's conference report describes the same old manner differently: in *syyia tardyy* "one rhythmic stroke on the tip of the khomus lamella corresponded to one syllable of the articulated text or sung tune". — [Ivanova 2015, "Khomus as an instrument of cult rites of the indigenous peoples of Yakutia", Lomonosov conference abstracts](https://conf.msu.ru/archive/Lomonosov_2015/data/6975/uid82785_report.pdf)
- `[DESCRIBED]` Markov's notation rule: "If the note group is covered with the sign 'tie' ... the finger beat should be made only at the first note of all the tied notes, the rest of notes are produced with the help of your speech apparatus and breath." — [Markov, Jew's Harp notation system](https://varganist.ru/491e.html)
- `[DESCRIBED]` Aronov's exercises count articulations per stroke: one half-smile per stroke, then "gradually increase the number of half-smiles per one stroke"; lip closings and openings "on one stroke. Try to increase the number of movements"; the vowel pairs "ИУ - ОА" with "one stroke per pair of sounds". — [Aronov, Nizhny Novgorod vargan school](http://web.archive.org/web/20200225231341/http://www.vargan.ru:80/varganzal/varganschools/94-uprazhneniya-s-varganom-nizhegorodskaya-vargannaya-shkola)
- `[DESCRIBED]` Forum (Aksenty): even the plainest pendulum-like plucking "leaves endless room", because breath and articulation are two more sound-making devices; all three should be fully independent, and "between the strokes one can, and should, insert other sounds". — [oberton.pro forum, "Learning the fast stroke"](https://www.oberton.pro/forum/topic/2721-%D0%BD%D0%B0%D1%83%D1%87%D0%B8%D1%82%D1%8C%D1%81%D1%8F-%D0%B1%D1%8B%D1%81%D1%82%D1%80%D0%BE%D0%BC%D1%83-%D0%B1%D0%BE%D1%8E/)

**Vowels, diphthong sweeps and the words used for them**
- `[DESCRIBED]` Beginner instruction: "While the harp's tongue is in motion, silently pronounce the vowels (A-E-I-O-U)." — [Jew's Harp Guild, How to Play](http://www.jewsharpguild.org/play.html)
- `[DESCRIBED]` Sweep exercise: "While plucking slowly, move from your biggest 'O' to your smallest 'E'. You should here a series of notes comes and go as you slide by them." Useful consonant shapes: "D, G, K, L and T". — [Harman, Mouthing Off #2](http://www.jewsharpguild.org/whmo2.html)
- `[DESCRIBED]` "Bending": combine the vowels into glides "such as Oi-Ai-Ei-Ui"; with the throat opened as in a yawn and brought "back to neutral" the bend is "much deeper than the normal bending sounds". — [Didgeridoo Dojo, lesson 5: Bending the sound](https://www.didgeridoodojo.com/how-to-play-jaw-harp/bending-the-sound-of-your-jaw-harp/); [lesson 7: A sound we can't name](https://www.didgeridoodojo.com/how-to-play-jaw-harp/weird-jews-harp-sound/); [lesson 6: Opening your throat](https://www.didgeridoodojo.com/how-to-play-jaw-harp/jaws-harp-sounds/)
- `[DESCRIBED]` Vandré's melody exercise: form "u - ü - i - ü - u" silently; overtones rise from u to i and fall back; for low accompanying tones "u - o - a - o - u" with the lips left unchanged. — [Vandré, Spieltechnik](http://www.rvandre.de/spieltechnik.html)
- `[OBSERVED]` Vowel orders used as scales: P. P. Ogotoev's Sakha "articulatory scale", printed as "А-О--Ö-U-Y-Ä-Ü-I", and Kh. S. Ikhtisamov's "articulatory row" "А-О-У-Э-И", both with "A" or "O" placed "at the very bottom of the scale", as reported in the Russian companion article. — [Nikolsky, Alekseyev, Alekseev & Dyakonova 2020, "How, where and when authentic traditions of Jaw Harp music of Siberia and the Far East have been forming. Part 1", Languages and Folklore of Indigenous Peoples of Siberia 2020 no. 1, pp. 9-32](https://fehu.philology.nsc.ru/journals/ykns/pdf/YFKNS_40/Alexeev_2020_9_32.pdf)
- `[OBSERVED]` Balinese genggong: the five pitches "ning nong neng nung nang" are produced by whispering the vowels "i o e u a" into the instrument. — [Morgan 2008, ch. 5.2](https://facultystaff.richmond.edu/~amcgraw/gamelan/ubc_2008_fall_morgan_deirdre-libre.pdf)
- `[MEASURED]` The four "poles" of Sakha khomus articulation, demonstrated by Ivan Alekseyev: "2.4-3.1 kHz for 'front,' 0.3-1.6 kHz for 'back,' 1.2-2.5 kHz for 'high,' and 0.6-0.9 kHz for 'low' vowels" (the register that is salient in each). Physical side: "front" syllables "require tension in throat, face, lips, jaw, cheeks and tongue"; "back" syllables "require relaxation in vocal apparatus"; "high" syllables resemble front ones "except that facial muscles remain relaxed", like smiling to oneself; "low" syllables "strongly activate the soft palate, configuring it into a 'cupola'" (players liken it to a big church bell). — [Nikolsky et al. 2020, the comparative audio demonstration of the four articulations and Examples 19-22](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.03051/full)
- `[DESCRIBED]` Players' own onomatopoeia for sweeps: "an outright pyau-vau" ("пяу-вау", from changing the sound with the diaphragm without exhaling); "a silent vaaaaau-vaauuuuu" ("беззвучное вааааау-ваауууууу"); syllables to speak while playing: "ori-ori, ari-ari, yori-yori, uri-uri, la-la-la, lo-lo-lo". — [oberton.pro forum, "Which techniques do you use when playing?"](https://www.oberton.pro/forum/topic/540-%D0%BA%D0%B0%D0%BA%D0%B8%D0%B5-%D1%82%D0%B5%D1%85%D0%BD%D0%B8%D0%BA%D0%B8-%D0%B2%D1%8B-%D0%B8%D1%81%D0%BF%D0%BE%D0%BB%D1%8C%D0%B7%D1%83%D0%B5%D1%82%D0%B5-%D0%BF%D1%80%D0%B8-%D0%B8%D0%B3%D1%80%D0%B5/)
- `[CLAIMED]` The morsing "is also used for making comical sound effects - wobbles, boings and onomatopoeic springing sounds" (journalist's words for Pirashanna Thevarajah's demonstration). — [Classic FM: What is a 'morsing'?](https://www.classicfm.com/discover-music/instruments/morsing-introduction-tuning-use/)

**Tongue taps, trills and syllables that chop the resonance without a new pluck**
- `[DESCRIBED]` Vandré: tongue articulation "works like articulation on the flute", except that "the tongue opens and closes the palatal cavity, the resonance space in the mouth" instead of an air stream. Slow sequences: tongue tip at the front palate "as if speaking Dü Dü Dü". Fast sequences: "Dü Dl Dü Dl Dü Dl, a kind of rocking motion of the front tongue". Flute tonguings "Dü Gü Dü Gü" and "Dü Rü Dü Rü" are mentioned as alternatives (after Quantz 1752). In sound example 7 the plucking is even and "within this basic rhythm" he articulates "Dü Dü and Dü Dl Dü. This technique is simple and can be played very fast." — [Vandré, Spieltechnik](http://www.rvandre.de/spieltechnik.html)
- `[CLAIMED]` (snippet) The English version of the same manual words it "de de de" and "doodle doodle doodle, using a swinging move of the tongue on the palate"; a retailer's instruction leaflet has "düdüdü". Neither page could be opened. — [danmoi.com: Vandré, Play the Jaw Harp Like a Virtuoso](https://www.danmoi.com/jews-harp-playing-instructions-play-the-jews-harp-like-a-virtuoso-robert-vandre-english_1); [Tchibo leaflet (PDF)](https://www.tchibo.ch/newmedia/document/488ad228c769410e/anleitung.pdf)
- `[DESCRIBED]` *The Quick Tongue*: "A quick up and down movement of the tongue's tip can trill between several high harmonics. This works well on a sustained pluck as the energy fades. Move the tongue as if saying 'T' or 'D' or 'LA'". With the throat closed by the back of the tongue "as if about to say the letter 'K'", the free tip bounces "off the roof of the mouth, producing the high 'LA-LA' sound. Doing this rapidly and repeatedly produces a very pleasing trill". — [Harman, Mouthing Off #7](http://www.jewsharpguild.org/whmo7.html); [Harman, Mouthing Off #4](http://www.jewsharpguild.org/whmo4.html)
- `[DESCRIBED]` "try making the sound taka-taka-taka without the Jews Harp. Now practice this sound without a sound and while plucking". — [Didgeridoo Dojo, lesson 8: Tongue movements](https://www.didgeridoodojo.com/how-to-play-jaw-harp/tongue-movements/)
- `[DESCRIBED]` Aronov's plosive drills: speak, then play one stroke per syllable, "ТУ-ТО-ТА-ТЕ-ТИ-ТЫ", "ДУ-ДО-ДА-ДЕ-ДИ-ДЫ", "ТРУ-ТРО-ТРА-ТРЕ-ТРИ-ТРЫ" (tongue strike on T, vibration on R), "КУ-КО-КА-КЭ-КИ-КЫ". "In fast, dynamic playing sounds such as 'K', 'T' and the rolled 'R' are good expressive means"; with them one can "set rhythmic lines, underline the strong beats, fill the rhythms". For "T" the tongue "strikes, touching the upper alveoli". — [Aronov, Nizhny Novgorod vargan school](http://web.archive.org/web/20200225231341/http://www.vargan.ru:80/varganzal/varganschools/94-uprazhneniya-s-varganom-nizhegorodskaya-vargannaya-shkola)
- `[DESCRIBED]` A flautist on the forum: as on the recorder one can pronounce "da da da do do do" or "ta ta to to". Another player: press the tongue tip to the palate right behind the upper teeth and play "opening and closing the larynx". — [oberton.pro forum, "Which techniques do you use when playing?"](https://www.oberton.pro/forum/topic/540-%D0%BA%D0%B0%D0%BA%D0%B8%D0%B5-%D1%82%D0%B5%D1%85%D0%BD%D0%B8%D0%BA%D0%B8-%D0%B2%D1%8B-%D0%B8%D1%81%D0%BF%D0%BE%D0%BB%D1%8C%D0%B7%D1%83%D0%B5%D1%82%D0%B5-%D0%BF%D1%80%D0%B8-%D0%B8%D0%B3%D1%80%D0%B5/)
- `[DESCRIBED]` Velum and side-tongue effects: saying voiceless "ungungungung" (the tongue closing the mouth at the soft palate) "produces an interesting effect"; a second effect comes when the middle of the tongue lies against the upper molars and the tip alternately touches and leaves the palate "as if saying 'nanana'" (sound example 10). — [Vandré, Spieltechnik](http://www.rvandre.de/spieltechnik.html)
- `[CLAIMED]` Morsing: "Movement of the player's tongue with constant plucking can produce very fast patterns of sound"; the mridangam syllables are not voiced: "you don't actually make sound of reciting the syllable but just move your tongue that way". The Wikipedia article carries a reliability notice. — [en.wikipedia: Morsing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morsing)
- `[OBSERVED]` Morsing: "the solkattu syllables are enunciated into the instrument in a kind of projected whisper" (from a personal communication with Jim Santi Owen, 2008). — [Morgan 2008, pp. 29-30](https://facultystaff.richmond.edu/~amcgraw/gamelan/ubc_2008_fall_morgan_deirdre-libre.pdf)

**Vibrato (slow periodic wobble of the mouth volume)**
- `[DESCRIBED]` Forum (Aksenty): there are several ways to make vibrato, including ones "with the soft palate" or "with the tongue turned 90 degrees"; the principle is always "frequent changes of the volume of the mouth cavity, close to the trilled sounds of phonetics"; he advises moving the effort from the tongue to the muscles under the tongue (the floor of the mouth). A learner reports two failed results: a forward-back tongue movement "with a characteristic 'yo-yo' overtone" ("ё-ё") and a trembling "more like the 'lark' technique, when the tongue tip goes up and down without touching the palate". — [oberton.pro forum, "Vibrato"](https://www.oberton.pro/forum/topic/3246-%D0%B2%D0%B8%D0%B1%D1%80%D0%B0%D1%82%D0%BE/)
- `[DESCRIBED]` Other vibratos: rapid alternation of inhale and exhale (Harman, section 4); a "rapid opening and closing of the throat, like rolling your 'R's in Spanish, produces a bassy, and difficult to control, vibrato"; Nadishana's finger taps on the lips (section 1); Aronov names a "throat vibrato" as an advanced technique built on larynx training. — [Harman, Mouthing Off #4](http://www.jewsharpguild.org/whmo4.html); [Aronov, Nizhny Novgorod vargan school](http://web.archive.org/web/20200225231341/http://www.vargan.ru:80/varganzal/varganschools/94-uprazhneniya-s-varganom-nizhegorodskaya-vargannaya-shkola)

**Lips, mouth corner, cheeks and jaw**
- `[DESCRIBED]` Aronov, *external articulation*: "it is enough to smile a little with a corner of the mouth"; the slit changes how the sound spreads in the mouth. *Closing and opening the lips*: starting with the lips lifted off the frame, strike and "slowly close the lips"; the technique "changes the sound very strongly and adds volume", because the incoming air stream changes shape and because the lips "take on part of the vibration of the body of the instrument". — [Aronov, Nizhny Novgorod vargan school](http://web.archive.org/web/20200225231341/http://www.vargan.ru:80/varganzal/varganschools/94-uprazhneniya-s-varganom-nizhegorodskaya-vargannaya-shkola)
- `[DESCRIBED]` With the harp mounted on a weight (no tooth contact) "the lips can be opened and closed while playing" (sound example 13). — [Vandré, Spieltechnik](http://www.rvandre.de/spieltechnik.html)
- `[DESCRIBED]` "The mouth, principally the tongue but also cheek and jaw, can be used as a second bellows, usually just for exhaling. The sound is very different than a diaphragm push as the size of the mouth changes rapidly here." — [Harman, Mouthing Off #4](http://www.jewsharpguild.org/whmo4.html)
- `[DESCRIBED]` Forum players name the Adam's apple ("кадык") as a favourite modifier ("a very interesting effect") and note that slightly open lips give a quieter sound than closed lips. — [oberton.pro forum, "Which techniques do you use when playing?"](https://www.oberton.pro/forum/topic/540-%D0%BA%D0%B0%D0%BA%D0%B8%D0%B5-%D1%82%D0%B5%D1%85%D0%BD%D0%B8%D0%BA%D0%B8-%D0%B2%D1%8B-%D0%B8%D1%81%D0%BF%D0%BE%D0%BB%D1%8C%D0%B7%D1%83%D0%B5%D1%82%D0%B5-%D0%BF%D1%80%D0%B8-%D0%B8%D0%B3%D1%80%D0%B5/)

**Pharynx and larynx as a slow sweep**
- `[DESCRIBED]` Aronov, *widening and narrowing the larynx* (the whole pharyngeal muscle group): exercise 1, larynx closed, "on the stroke open the pharynx, trying to enlarge it as far as possible"; exercise 2, start fully open and "on the stroke slowly contract the pharynx so that the changes in the sound are as smooth as possible"; both with the breath held. *Raising and lowering a vowel*: fix the highest "O", then "over successive strokes" go to the lowest by lowering the larynx; repeat with "У, А, Э, И, Ы". — [Aronov, Nizhny Novgorod vargan school](http://web.archive.org/web/20200225231341/http://www.vargan.ru:80/varganzal/varganschools/94-uprazhneniya-s-varganom-nizhegorodskaya-vargannaya-shkola)
- `[DESCRIBED]` Shishigin on the very low Sakha sound "Кенкелей" ("throat-and-lung"): enlarge the mouth cavity as far as possible by setting the speech organs and vocal cords for "O"; for a still lower sound add "the region of the larynx and the chest cavity"; "very hard to explain". — [Shishigin interview](https://varganist.ru/53.html)
- `[DESCRIBED]` "You can close your throat off way down low, as if you had been hit in the stomach and were now choking ... This accents the hollowness". Tongue-channel shapes: Aronov sets the tongue for "Щ" or between "Щ" and "С" and takes one long inhale or exhale over several strokes, "so one can find whistling sounds". — [Harman, Mouthing Off #4](http://www.jewsharpguild.org/whmo4.html); [Aronov, Nizhny Novgorod vargan school](http://web.archive.org/web/20200225231341/http://www.vargan.ru:80/varganzal/varganschools/94-uprazhneniya-s-varganom-nizhegorodskaya-vargannaya-shkola)

**Named imitations and how each is made**
- `[DESCRIBED]` *Lark's song* (Sakha): "produced by quick tongue movements back and forth"; pitch is varied by singing "syllables like a lark: iy ia io iu". — [Markov, "Varvara Stepanova's master class"](https://varganist.ru/43e.html)
- `[OBSERVED]` The lark imitation (*kuoregei*, "associated with sunrise") is used together with rounded vowels, either because the lips are needed anyway or for the association of rounding with surprise. Ivanova glosses the related term "куорэгэйдэтии" as "nightingale trill". — [Nikolsky et al. 2020 (Russian Part 1), section 3](https://fehu.philology.nsc.ru/journals/ykns/pdf/YFKNS_40/Alexeev_2020_9_32.pdf); [Nikolsky et al. 2020, footnote 15](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.03051/full); [Ivanova 2015](https://conf.msu.ru/archive/Lomonosov_2015/data/6975/uid82785_report.pdf)
- `[DESCRIBED]` *Goose squawk*: "quick touches of the tip of the tongue to the palate"; the pitch is moved smoothly by "displacing the point of contact of the tongue from the teeth to the soft palate". — [Markov, "Varvara Stepanova's master class"](https://varganist.ru/43e.html)
- `[DESCRIBED]` *Cuckoo*: "The cuckoo word should pronounced by syllables Ka-Ku", trying higher and deeper versions. Shishigin: a rather low instrument suits it, "at least F"; his best cuckoo khomus is "between E and F". — [Markov, "Varvara Stepanova's master class"](https://varganist.ru/43e.html); [Shishigin interview](https://varganist.ru/53.html)
- `[DESCRIBED]` *Horse*: lamella damped by the left hand, three fingers of the right hand striking in turn (section 1). *Drops*: lamella pressed at mid-length, struck from both sides, "side tones by breath and by movements of your tongue" (section 2). Aronov adds tongue clicks to the damped strokes for dripping. — [Markov, "Varvara Stepanova's master class"](https://varganist.ru/43e.html); [Aronov, Nizhny Novgorod vargan school](http://web.archive.org/web/20200225231341/http://www.vargan.ru:80/varganzal/varganschools/94-uprazhneniya-s-varganom-nizhegorodskaya-vargannaya-shkola)
- `[OBSERVED]` A Sakha piece on the summer festival typically strings together the round-dance rhythm formula, a crescendo "as if gradually approaching the festival", *kuoregei*, *kebe* ("cuckoo call, announcing the onset of summer"), *tabygyr* and *uyuu* ("articulation of words representing shouting of excited people and singing of dance-leaders"). — [Nikolsky et al. 2020, footnote 15](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.03051/full)
- `[OBSERVED]` Other imitation repertoires: Khalkha Mongol women teach children "using imitations of horse and camel gaits"; Tuvan khomus playing includes "imitation of sounds of nature"; the Sakha khomus "can produce the different sound of nature including dripping of melting ice, birds' chirping, the clatter of horse hooves". — [Nikolsky et al. 2020, footnote 41](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.03051/full); [Tadagawa 2017, The New Research of Tuva no. 2](https://nit.tuva.asia/nit/article/download/718/1107)

### Inferences
- (Mine.) All gestures in this section act on the resonance (the filter), not on the lamella, and the sources explicitly place them inside a ringing pluck. They are class A. The engine needs, per hit, a small set of time-shaped filter gestures: a one-way glide (up or down), a there-and-back glide, a periodic wobble (vibrato / lark), and a gate pattern (taps that close and reopen the cavity one to several times).
- (Mine.) Because the lamella keeps ringing behind a tongue tap, a tap closes only the upper resonance; the drone and any low throat resonance continue. This differs audibly (per Vandré's flute comparison) from a new pluck, which adds a fresh attack. A synth should not model tongue taps as re-triggers.
- (Mine.) The lark and the goose differ in the mechanism the sources give: lark = tongue moving without touching (a continuous wobble of a high formant), goose = tongue tip touching the palate (a gated, tapped sound whose pitch follows the contact point). Cuckoo = two discrete vowel targets (high then low).
- (Mine.) Speed limits can only be borrowed from speech: the syllable trains named here (de-de-de, taka-taka, dü-dl) are ordinary speech articulations, so their rates are bounded by speech articulation rates. No jaw harp source states a number, so any default sweep time is a design choice to be tuned by ear.

### Gaps
- No source gives a duration in milliseconds for an upward or downward sweep, a trill rate, or a vibrato rate. This is the largest gap for the engine.
- The exact words "doing / dojng", "twang" (as a sound name), "yoi", "ee-oo", "da-ga-da-ga", "tiki-taka" were not found in any source read; the documented equivalents are listed above.
- How wind is imitated was not found (wind is only named among the things imitated). Ivan Alekseyev's CD "The Yakut khomus World" is said to contain "14 basic ways of playing the Yakut khomus" ([Markov's review](https://varganist.ru/42e.html)); the list itself was not obtained, nor was Shishigin's book "Играйте на хомусе" (khomus.ru unreachable).
- Vladiswar Nadishana's own systematic description of techniques was not found; only the two techniques Vandré credits to him and a retailer interview (snippet) in which he says he applies "all my playing techniques" on small kohkins ([danmoi.com interview](https://www.danmoi.com/nadishana-whilst-playing-the-jaw-harp-i-imagine-a-whole-orchestra)).

## 4. Breath articulations: accents, in/out breathing locked to the plucks, breath alone driving the lamella, panting

### Takeaway
Breath is the second sound source of the instrument: air through the gap makes the lamella louder (mainly the fundamental), can keep it ringing or even start it without a pluck, and, pulsed by the diaphragm, gives accents and whole rhythms between the plucks. Teaching texts drill fixed breath-to-pluck patterns (one breath per pluck, two or three per pluck, a pluck every second breath), and fast playing in several traditions alternates inhale and exhale on successive notes.

### Cited Findings

**What breath does to the sound**
- `[DESCRIBED]` "Articulated notes, set off against each other, can be brought about by fast (or slow) breath pushes. The air flowing in and out mainly reinforces the fundamental." In sound example 6 the plucking is regular and "the breath sets rasping accents between the plucks". — [Vandré, Spieltechnik](http://www.rvandre.de/spieltechnik.html)
- `[DESCRIBED]` "Breathing through a Jew's harp gives it presence and makes the sound of a closed throat really stand out." "Inhaling and exhaling in rapid succession will add a vibrato effect. Hard blasts of air can overblow the reed causing a kind of base distortion." — [Harman, Mouthing Off #4](http://www.jewsharpguild.org/whmo4.html); [Harman, Mouthing Off #2](http://www.jewsharpguild.org/whmo2.html)
- `[DESCRIBED]` "Play while breathing in and out; the sound becomes much louder." — [oberton.pro, Basics of Jaw Harp Playing](https://www.oberton.pro/en/info/play.htm)
- `[DESCRIBED]` Breath direction colours the sound: "note that on the inhale the sound rises and on the exhale it falls". — [Aronov, Nizhny Novgorod vargan school](http://web.archive.org/web/20200225231341/http://www.vargan.ru:80/varganzal/varganschools/94-uprazhneniya-s-varganom-nizhegorodskaya-vargannaya-shkola)
- `[CLAIMED]` German Wikipedia: "By simultaneously blowing or drawing air through, this sound is amplified." Hungarian Wikipedia: the fundamental "can be amplified by drawing in and blowing out air, or turned into a rhythmic accompaniment by the pulsating movement of the diaphragm; in this case the instrument also works as a free reed". — [de.wikipedia: Maultrommel](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maultrommel); [hu.wikipedia: Doromb](https://hu.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doromb)
- `[CLAIMED]` Czech Wikipedia: "By rhythmically drawing air through the gap while playing one gets the effect of one's own unaccented and colourful accompaniment, the so-called *kontrování*." — [cs.wikipedia: Brumle](https://cs.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brumle)

**Breath alone starting or sustaining the lamella**
- `[DESCRIBED]` "Some harps can be made to speak with only well directed air. The spaces between frame and reed must be very tight and usually the reed is thin and flexes easily." — [Harman, Mouthing Off #2](http://www.jewsharpguild.org/whmo2.html)
- `[DESCRIBED]` Vandré: the lamella starts to sound without plucking when air is drawn in or pushed out; as explained to him by Clemens Voigt, "the lamella must not lie quite in one plane with the frame, and the air stream must pull it back toward the frame". On a bow-shaped harp one can press the base of the lamella lightly with the thumb and draw air: "now the Maultrommel works like a harmonica" (sound example 14 compares harmonica, plucked harp and air-driven harp on the same note). — [Vandré, Töne der Maultrommel](http://www.rvandre.de/toene.html)
- `[CLAIMED]` Czech Wikipedia: "A good instrument sounds its fundamental even without plucking, merely by drawing air through the gap". — [cs.wikipedia: Brumle](https://cs.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brumle)
- `[CLAIMED]` Morsing: "Notes are sustained by the breath"; "a lot of air is needed to sustain notes and create resonance. You can breathe in or out through the instrument, something that creates sustained notes and also helps you make varied sounds." — [Classic FM: What is a 'morsing'?](https://www.classicfm.com/discover-music/instruments/morsing-introduction-tuning-use/)
- `[CLAIMED]` (snippet) A retailer describes đàn môi models whose vibration runs "short to very long with breathing technique" and which can be made to vibrate "only by sharply breathe through it". — [danmoi.com product listing](https://www.danmoi.com/jews-harps-jawharps/jews-harp-sets__Steel?ed=1)
- `[OBSERVED]` Bali has a separate breath-only instrument, the *enggung*, "a single free reed enclosed in a frame and activated solely by the breath"; in the ensemble "blown genggong" give "the sound of the big frog". — [Morgan 2008, ch. 4](https://facultystaff.richmond.edu/~amcgraw/gamelan/ubc_2008_fall_morgan_deirdre-libre.pdf)
- `[DESCRIBED]` A forum player: synchronising intense diaphragm breathing with the strokes "lets one swing the vargan up strongly, very effective sounds, as if playing a march". — [oberton.pro forum, "Which techniques do you use when playing?"](https://www.oberton.pro/forum/topic/540-%D0%BA%D0%B0%D0%BA%D0%B8%D0%B5-%D1%82%D0%B5%D1%85%D0%BD%D0%B8%D0%BA%D0%B8-%D0%B2%D1%8B-%D0%B8%D1%81%D0%BF%D0%BE%D0%BB%D1%8C%D0%B7%D1%83%D0%B5%D1%82%D0%B5-%D0%BF%D1%80%D0%B8-%D0%B8%D0%B3%D1%80%D0%B5/)

**Breath locked to the plucks (taught patterns)**
- `[DESCRIBED]` Aronov, *breathing oscillations* (schemes are his): (1) "stroke-inhale - stroke-exhale - stroke-inhale - stroke-exhale..."; (2) stroke, short pause, "a sharp but noiseless inhale with the belly (a push of the diaphragm on the inhale)", stroke, diaphragm push on the exhale: "stroke - inhale - stroke - exhale", gradually faster; (3) "stroke-inhale - exhale-inhale-exhale - stroke-inhale-exhale"; (4) "stroke-exhale - inhale - stroke-exhale"; (5) "stroke-inhale - exhale - stroke-inhale"; (6) 4 and 5 joined into a cycle, accelerating; (7) "on one stroke two exhales, on the next stroke two inhales". Inhale and exhale are to be "equal in volume and force". — [Aronov, Nizhny Novgorod vargan school](http://web.archive.org/web/20200225231341/http://www.vargan.ru:80/varganzal/varganschools/94-uprazhneniya-s-varganom-nizhegorodskaya-vargannaya-shkola)
- `[DESCRIBED]` Aronov, *tremors of the diaphragm*, explained through laughter: "the diaphragm pushes air out in a series of sharp contractions. If each such contraction is followed by a short sharp inhale, these are tremors." Exercises: one push-out plus an equal sharp inhale per stroke; "on one stroke three short and sharp inhales and exhales"; stroke, short pause, then "a sharp exhale, then inhale and at once exhale", all on one stroke, amplitude reduced "to the limit". — [Aronov, Nizhny Novgorod vargan school](http://web.archive.org/web/20200225231341/http://www.vargan.ru:80/varganzal/varganschools/94-uprazhneniya-s-varganom-nizhegorodskaya-vargannaya-shkola)
- `[DESCRIBED]` Harman: "Exhaling at varying pressures in a rhythmic pattern produces a rhythmic and tone change in the sound. Exhaling or inhaling in a start/stop pattern allows a time to make the closed throat sound. Alternation inhale/exhale rapidly produces a vibrato effect. Experiments in combinations of these simple patterns can lead to intricate rhythms." — [Harman, Mouthing Off #4](http://www.jewsharpguild.org/whmo4.html)
- `[DESCRIBED]` Beginner combinations: "breathe in-pluck, breath out-pluck, or breath in breath out then pluck." — [Didgeridoo Dojo, lesson 9: How to breathe](https://www.didgeridoodojo.com/?p=13388)
- `[DESCRIBED]` Markov's notation marks breath on every note: "The arrow pointing upwards means exhalation, downwards one means inhalation"; each rhythm exercise is to be played twice, "giving the rhythm by plucking the reed, and by plucking the reed and breath". — [Markov, Jew's Harp notation system](https://varganist.ru/491e.html); [Markov, Exercises](https://varganist.ru/49e.html)
- `[OBSERVED]` Genggong: "Many Jew's harp traditions employ the use of breathing in and out in rapid succession, as it keeps the momentum going on the instrument and allows for faster playing." In sixteenth-note interlocking patterns "half the notes are played while exhaling and the other half are played by inhaling"; in slower melodic playing "the notes are all generally sounded by exhaling". The exhaling technique is called *ngengkahin*. — [Morgan 2008, ch. 5.2](https://facultystaff.richmond.edu/~amcgraw/gamelan/ubc_2008_fall_morgan_deirdre-libre.pdf)
- `[DESCRIBED]` Sakha "throat style" ("БЭЛЭС ДОР5ООНО"): "a strike, then a sloe [slow] inhalation, then a hard exhalation with guttural side tone." — [Markov, "Varvara Stepanova's master class"](https://varganist.ru/43e.html)
- `[CLAIMED]` Morsing: the syllable "/ŋg/ or a variant thereof, is sounded through the nose while air is pushed out or pulled in through the mouth". — [en.wikipedia: Morsing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morsing)

**Panting**
- `[DESCRIBED]` Forum: breathing as in the singers' warm-up called "the little dog", "very fast inhales and exhales", gives what the player calls "speed vargan"; combined with fast two-way plucking it is very unusual, but to avoid "porridge" one must keep "a definite number of strokes on the inhale and a definite number of strokes on the exhale". Another player: "even if the strokes on the lamella are very measured, the playing is sped up many times by fast inhales and exhales." — [oberton.pro forum, "Lower breathing"](https://www.oberton.pro/forum/topic/733-%D0%BD%D0%B8%D0%B6%D0%BD%D0%B5%D0%B5-%D0%B4%D1%8B%D1%85%D0%B0%D0%BD%D0%B8%D0%B5/); [oberton.pro forum, "Learning the fast stroke"](https://www.oberton.pro/forum/topic/2721-%D0%BD%D0%B0%D1%83%D1%87%D0%B8%D1%82%D1%8C%D1%81%D1%8F-%D0%B1%D1%8B%D1%81%D1%82%D1%80%D0%BE%D0%BC%D1%83-%D0%B1%D0%BE%D1%8E/)
- `[CLAIMED]` (snippet) The Brüder Mayr method book for Maultrommel has a chapter "Hecheln, das Spiel mit dem Atem" (panting, playing with the breath). Only the chapter title is known; the page was not readable. — [Sheet Music Plus listing: Maultrommel - Eine Spielanleitung der Brüder Mayr](https://www.sheetmusicplus.com/en/product/maultrommel-eine-spielanleitung-der-bruder-mayr-21048785.html)
- `[CLAIMED]` (snippet) A generic how-to page suggests "saying 'ha-ha-ha' while plucking to create a triple rhythm". The page looks machine-generated; treat as unreliable. — [m.fightcancer.org: How To Play The Jew's Harp](https://m.fightcancer.org/play-jews-harp)

### Inferences
- (Mine.) A breath pulse during a ring is class A (it re-energises or swells the same note and adds noise and fundamental); a breath pattern that alternates per pluck (inhale notes / exhale notes) is class B, but it needs a per-hit parameter in the engine (breath direction and strength of this hit), because inhale and exhale notes differ in colour (Aronov) and in accent (Morgan, section 6).
- (Mine.) "One stroke, three sharp inhale-exhale pairs" and similar drills mean that a single hit can carry a short rhythm of two to six breath pulses. An engine that wants this vocabulary needs a per-hit "breath pulse count" or a tempo-synced breath tremolo that keeps running while the lamella rings.
- (Mine.) Since breath can start and sustain the lamella on tight-gapped instruments, a breath pulse on a nearly silent lamella is a soft-attack note with no pluck transient. This is a distinct hit type (air-started note), plausibly useful as a pad between drum-like hits.

### Gaps
- No rates: how fast players pant (breaths per second) or how long a breath swell takes is not stated anywhere.
- Explicit "ha" / "hu" syllables for exhaled accents were found only in Markov's notation ("ha", glossed as a glottal/epiglottal onset, section 5) and in one unreliable page; the reliable teaching texts describe diaphragm pushes without a syllable.
- The Mayr brothers' chapter on panting, Wolf Janscha's chapters on "metric breathing", and Áron Szilágyi's breathing lessons were not readable (see section 7).
- The đàn môi breath technique rests on a retailer snippet only; Trần Quang Hải's writing on technique was not found.

## 5. Glottis and throat: closed and open sounds, glottal gates, throat pulsing, voice

### Takeaway
Closing the glottis (or sealing the mouth from the throat with the back of the tongue) is a basic two-state switch in melodic styles: "closed" notes sound sharp and clear and favour one half of the overtone series, "open" notes are breathed. The same closure, opened and shut in rhythm ("K", a cough, "ha"/"ah"), is used as an on/off gate for accents and counter-rhythms. Voice is added as humming, throat singing or actual speech through the instrument.

### Cited Findings

**Open and closed sounds as a note-by-note switch**
- `[DESCRIBED]` Markov: "Open sounds ... are played just with breath, not stopping anything in the mouth." "Closed sounds can be produced by different ways, but the best one is to close your glottis. Closed notes should be played without breathing." To find the closure: "light coughing, hiccupping, laughing". Rule: "All even numbers [harmonics] are played by an open sound, and odd ones are played by a closed." Kyrgyz temir-komuz players "constantly rotate open and closed sounds". — [Markov, "Finding the Jew's harp Notes I", varganist.ru](https://varganist.ru/46e.html)
- `[DESCRIBED]` Markov (Russian course text): with the glottis shut "the vargan sounds sharp, beautiful, full"; open sounds should be "free of the slightest vowel colouring" and closed ones "very clean and bright"; practice syllable for the closure on a short exhale: a distinct "[кэк]". In his major-like scale from the 8th harmonic, the degrees 1 to 7 are open, closed, open, closed, open, closed, closed. — [Markov, Russian course text on open and closed sounds, "hissing" solfège and the vargan scale, varganist.ru](https://varganist.ru/42.html)
- `[MEASURED]` Vandré's own spectrum comparison (self-published, one instrument in G, overtones 7 to 15 in his numbering, recorded open with a little breath, with closed glottis, and closed at the palate with "ng"): "Only with open playing can the odd-numbered overtones be made clearly audible. The closed ways of playing 'palate' and 'glottis' amplify the even-numbered overtones very clearly, the odd ones insufficiently". He notes that Norwegian traditional players choose open ("åpen") or closed ("lukket") note by note, as taught in a Norsk Munnharpeforum course. — [Vandré, Töne der Maultrommel, section "Offener und geschlossener Vokaltrakt"](http://www.rvandre.de/toene.html)
- `[DESCRIBED]` "Two distinct note ranges are available": the open throat with air ("the most used range"), expandable to "a cavernous sound" by opening as wide as possible without air; and the throat closed off "with the very back of your tongue", where "pitch control is much easier ... and the highest harmonics can be reached. Rhythmic alterations between these two modes offers unlimited possibilities." — [Harman, Mouthing Off #7](http://www.jewsharpguild.org/whmo7.html)
- `[OBSERVED]` "Compared with other Jew's harp playing techniques, genggong uses an open glottis ... That is what gives genggong its characteristically deep and guttural sound"; the same partials can be sounded with a closed glottis, but "the resonance is totally different ... and open glottis playing is much louder because of the extra resonating cavity". — [Morgan 2008, ch. 5.2](https://facultystaff.richmond.edu/~amcgraw/gamelan/ubc_2008_fall_morgan_deirdre-libre.pdf)
- `[CLAIMED]` A Norwegian online course promises to teach "the mysterious 'closed' tones ... the stomp, the strokes". — [The Folk Music Academy: Norwegian Jaws Harp, Tom Willy Rustad](https://live.thefolkmusicacademy.com/courses/21-norwegian-jaws-harp-no)
- `[CLAIMED]` English Wikipedia lists "in some traditions closing the glottis" among the ways of changing the sound. — [en.wikipedia: Jew's harp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jew%27s_harp)

**The glottis or the back of the tongue as a rhythmic gate**
- `[DESCRIBED]` Vandré: with the glottis closed "considerably lower tones of the Maultrommel become clearly audible" in addition to the normal ones; it "can also be used for articulation". He explains the closure by the cough, where the glottis "suddenly releases the air pressed by the abdominal muscles". In sound example 9 a melody is joined by "a deeper second voice" when the glottis closes, plus "rhythmic accents through the glottis". — [Vandré, Spieltechnik, section Klangeffekte](http://www.rvandre.de/spieltechnik.html)
- `[DESCRIBED]` "The air flow can be opened and closed sharply by closing of the throat with the base of your tongue as in saying the letter 'K'. This on-off switch is very effective way to add additional rhythms and counterpoint." "The back of your throat can block the diaphragm pressure and release it in a sudden burst, like saying 'K'. The letter 'T' can be used as well ... the exhale mode is most effective here and the inhale mode is still in the 'experimental' stages." — [Harman, Mouthing Off #2](http://www.jewsharpguild.org/whmo2.html); [Harman, Mouthing Off #4](http://www.jewsharpguild.org/whmo4.html)
- `[DESCRIBED]` Markov's signs "ha" (abrupt beginning) and "ah" (sudden stop), both "with the help of an epiglottis". — [Markov, Jew's Harp notation system](https://varganist.ru/491e.html)
- `[DESCRIBED]` Forum players: "sharp inhales and exhales, with the same switching on/off of the air passing through the larynx, so that it opens and closes instantly ... in turn, at a fast rhythm"; after about a minute "a double sound begins ... two lines of sound are heard", and listeners ask where the second vargan is hidden. Another: the tongue tip pressed behind the upper teeth, "opening and closing the larynx". — [oberton.pro forum, "Which techniques do you use when playing?"](https://www.oberton.pro/forum/topic/540-%D0%BA%D0%B0%D0%BA%D0%B8%D0%B5-%D1%82%D0%B5%D1%85%D0%BD%D0%B8%D0%BA%D0%B8-%D0%B2%D1%8B-%D0%B8%D1%81%D0%BF%D0%BE%D0%BB%D1%8C%D0%B7%D1%83%D0%B5%D1%82%D0%B5-%D0%BF%D1%80%D0%B8-%D0%B8%D0%B3%D1%80%D0%B5/)
- `[DESCRIBED]` Aronov alternates closed and open vowels in drills: closed "И" and "О", one stroke each, breath held; open "О - И" on the inhale and "И - О" on the exhale. — [Aronov, Nizhny Novgorod vargan school](http://web.archive.org/web/20200225231341/http://www.vargan.ru:80/varganzal/varganschools/94-uprazhneniya-s-varganom-nizhegorodskaya-vargannaya-shkola)
- `[OBSERVED]` "JH articulations require constant control of all the constituents of the vocal apparatus, and reconfigurations of larynx and pharynx are usually done automatically - based on the reflexes established by speech" (Ivan Alekseev's remark). A cited analysis of Chuvan frame jaw harp music distinguishes four layers: the fundamental; "its subharmonic chest resonance an octave below"; a middle melodic layer "controlled by the player's breathing and tongue position, exhibiting 'talking' qualities"; and a high melodic layer of sustained formants "which resembles singing". — [Nikolsky et al. 2020, footnotes 42 and 62](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.03051/full)

**Voice with the instrument**
- `[DESCRIBED]` "Try sounding a note in your throat while you play - the basis of what is often called throat singing - and blend the two sounds." — [oberton.pro, Basics of Jaw Harp Playing](https://www.oberton.pro/en/info/play.htm)
- `[DESCRIBED]` Harman on singing through the harp: two "frequency generators" give intervals; matching voice and harp "can be hauntingly beautiful"; slightly detuning creates "beats"; "Soft reeds are more influenced by voice and air". "Since air must eventually be inhaled, vocal techniques are a natural to combine with a non-vocal accent." — [Harman, Mouthing Off #4](http://www.jewsharpguild.org/whmo4.html); [Harman, Mouthing Off #7](http://www.jewsharpguild.org/whmo7.html)
- `[OBSERVED]` Tuvan ways of playing the khomus include "silent execution of the 'words'" of well-known songs, "called chugaaladyp oinaary or 'speaking play'", and "simultaneous execution of the khomus playing and throat-singing". — [Tadagawa 2017](https://nit.tuva.asia/nit/article/download/718/1107)
- `[OBSERVED]` Sakha *syyia tardy*'s closeness to the sung *toyuk* "enables the simultaneous singing and playing of a JH"; Shishigin says that a player in this manner "can also hum to himself" a just-composed toyuk. — [Nikolsky et al. 2020](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.03051/full); [Shishigin interview](https://varganist.ru/53.html)
- `[CLAIMED]` Morsing: some players "speak into the instruments while playing, thus giving it the effect of a light haunting echo". Sakha: "The 'talking vargan' method is used by some lyrical storytellers, who are able to pronounce words while playing the khomus." — [en.wikipedia: Morsing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morsing); [en.wikipedia: Jew's harp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jew%27s_harp)
- `[CLAIMED]` A journalist on Damon Salinas's "techno" jaw harp: "Different tones and notes are achieved through mouth shape, cheek formation, and voice." — [Laughing Squid: Musician Plays Techno Music on Jaw Harp](https://laughingsquid.com/techno-music-on-jaw-harp/)

### Inferences
- (Mine.) Vandré's "even-numbered overtones closed" and Markov's "odd harmonics closed" agree once the numbering is aligned: Vandré counts overtones from the first overtone (= 2nd harmonic), and his "10th overtone, about c sharp 3" on a G instrument (98 Hz) is the 11th harmonic. Both say: odd harmonics need the closed tract, even harmonics the open one.
- (Mine.) Open/closed is a per-note state (class A property of each hit), with a strong timbre difference: open = breath noise, stronger fundamental, both low and high resonances; closed = no breath, brighter and "clean", different low resonance. A glottal *gate* inside a note (closed-open-closed during one ring) is also class A and is how players get "two lines of sound" from one stream of plucks.
- (Mine.) Because a closed glottis removes air flow, breath accents (section 4) and closed sounds exclude each other at any instant; patterns alternate them. An engine should not allow full breath drive and "closed" colour at the same time.

### Gaps
- No source describes a voiced *growl* on the jaw harp as such (the didgeridoo sense); what is documented is humming/singing, throat singing and speech through the instrument.
- No text source on beatboxing combined with a jaw harp was found (see section 8).
- Timing of glottal gates (how short a closure can be) is not given anywhere.
- Vandré's measurement is one instrument, one player, self-published; no peer-reviewed measurement of open versus closed playing was read for this file.

## 6. Accents and dynamics: how an accent is made

### Takeaway
Teachers say plainly that loudness and accent come less from plucking harder than from the mouth and the air: an accent is typically a stack of a pluck, a plosive tongue or throat release and a diaphragm push, all at once. In genggong playing the accent pattern is tied to breath direction and the wrist snap, so that accented and unaccented ("passing") notes alternate.

### Cited Findings
- `[DESCRIBED]` Aronov's "triple strike": "finger on the lamella of the vargan + strike of the tongue on 'K' + strike of air, the diaphragm, pushing out. The result is a powerful explosive bass sound - the basis of the rhythm." With "T": each stroke accompanied by the tongue's "T" and "an active, sharp push of the diaphragm on the exhale": "stroke-exhale-'T' - stroke-exhale-'T'...". Plosives suit accenting "because of their percussive, powerful sound". — [Aronov, Nizhny Novgorod vargan school](http://web.archive.org/web/20200225231341/http://www.vargan.ru:80/varganzal/varganschools/94-uprazhneniya-s-varganom-nizhegorodskaya-vargannaya-shkola)
- `[DESCRIBED]` Aronov, exercise "ТЁ-ТЯ" on two strokes: with the first stroke "a sharp inhale (diaphragm, belly out)", with the second "a sharp exhale (diaphragm, belly in)". — [Aronov, Nizhny Novgorod vargan school](http://web.archive.org/web/20200225231341/http://www.vargan.ru:80/varganzal/varganschools/94-uprazhneniya-s-varganom-nizhegorodskaya-vargannaya-shkola)
- `[DESCRIBED]` Loudness is not pluck force: plucking harder is "usually ... not the case. Creating accurate mouth cavity sizes and efficient use of air flow over the reed will have a much greater effect". "The more accurately you match the mouth to the harmonic, the clearer the note." — [Harman, Mouthing Off #2](http://www.jewsharpguild.org/whmo2.html)
- `[DESCRIBED]` Accents placed between plucks by the breath ("rasping accents") and by the glottis ("rhythmic accents through the glottis"); the muted tongue "twang" as "a very nice accent"; a tongue-driven air push that ends by muting the lamella "accenting the tone shift which had just occurred". — [Vandré, Spieltechnik](http://www.rvandre.de/spieltechnik.html); [Harman, Mouthing Off #4](http://www.jewsharpguild.org/whmo4.html)
- `[CLAIMED]` (snippet) Retailer leaflet: the finger keeps plucking evenly while breath and/or tongue set the accents. — [Tchibo leaflet (PDF, not retrievable)](https://www.tchibo.ch/newmedia/document/488ad228c769410e/anleitung.pdf)
- `[OBSERVED]` Genggong: "some of the notes are accented while others are not." In a sixteenth-note pattern the notes on the exhale (ning, neng, nang) are accented and those on the inhale (nong, nung) are "unaccented, effectively acting as passing tones". "There are, then, two layers ... the overall melodic flow of sixteenth notes, and ... the rhythm that emerges as certain notes are accented and others are not." The accents come with the "loose but precise snapping of the wrist". — [Morgan 2008, ch. 5.2, Figure 5.5](https://facultystaff.richmond.edu/~amcgraw/gamelan/ubc_2008_fall_morgan_deirdre-libre.pdf)
- `[DESCRIBED]` Dynamics by stroke: an even two-way wrist stroke "with a gradual rise and fall of loudness" is a set exercise. Dynamics by breath: "the force of the inhale-exhale sets the loudness, and the frequency of breathing the rhythm" (a forum player's summary). Dynamics by lips: slightly open lips are quieter than closed lips. — [Aronov, Nizhny Novgorod vargan school](http://web.archive.org/web/20200225231341/http://www.vargan.ru:80/varganzal/varganschools/94-uprazhneniya-s-varganom-nizhegorodskaya-vargannaya-shkola); [oberton.pro forum, "Lower breathing"](https://www.oberton.pro/forum/topic/733-%D0%BD%D0%B8%D0%B6%D0%BD%D0%B5%D0%B5-%D0%B4%D1%8B%D1%85%D0%B0%D0%BD%D0%B8%D0%B5/); [oberton.pro forum, "Which techniques do you use when playing?"](https://www.oberton.pro/forum/topic/540-%D0%BA%D0%B0%D0%BA%D0%B8%D0%B5-%D1%82%D0%B5%D1%85%D0%BD%D0%B8%D0%BA%D0%B8-%D0%B2%D1%8B-%D0%B8%D1%81%D0%BF%D0%BE%D0%BB%D1%8C%D0%B7%D1%83%D0%B5%D1%82%D0%B5-%D0%BF%D1%80%D0%B8-%D0%B8%D0%B3%D1%80%D0%B5/)
- `[OBSERVED]` A crescendo "as if gradually approaching the festival" is a named expressive device in Sakha pieces. — [Nikolsky et al. 2020, footnote 15](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.03051/full)
- `[DESCRIBED]` Markov's notation has a dedicated sign for "Making an accent" and a sign each for strokes away from and toward the player. — [Markov, Jew's Harp notation system](https://varganist.ru/491e.html)

### Inferences
- (Mine.) An accent is a class A event with several coupled parts: pluck strength, a breath pulse at the attack, a plosive opening of the cavity (a fast upward opening of the filter from closed), and often a vowel choice (bass "explosive" accents with K plus diaphragm; bright accents with T). MIDI velocity should therefore drive more than amplitude: breath amount, brightness/opening speed and, at the top, the chance of frame clank.
- (Mine.) The unaccented class is as important as the accented one: Morgan's "passing notes" and Aronov's plain strokes between accents are quiet, breath-coloured notes with no plosive. This is the closest documented equivalent of ghost notes.
- (Mine.) Which syllable gives which accent colour ("K" + diaphragm = bass, per Aronov) is the only source statement linking a consonant to a spectral result; the rest of any velocity-to-timbre map is a design decision.

### Gaps
- No measurement of how much louder an accented stroke is (dB), or of the spectral difference between accented and plain strokes, was found in the technique literature.
- Morsing accent practice (how solkattu accents map to stroke, breath and tongue) was not obtained from a teaching source.

## 7. How teachers write or speak a rhythm; typical patterns; tempo ranges

### Takeaway
Three spoken systems carry jaw harp rhythm: ordinary speech syllables (T/K/D consonants plus vowels, "taka-taka", "dü dl"), the Carnatic percussion syllables (solkattu / konnakol) silently mouthed on the morsing, and Balinese solfège vowels on the genggong. Written systems range from Western notes re-read as overtone numbers with added signs for breath, stroke direction, accent, closed sound, glottal start/stop and damping (Markov), to number tabs and finger-number fingerings. Tempo figures are almost absent.

### Cited Findings

**Written notation**
- `[DESCRIBED]` Markov's system (used on varganist.ru): notes stand for overtone numbers, not pitches ("'C' of the first octave means the eight overtone"); "The rhythm is given by finger beats on the reed at each of not tied notes"; arrows up/down for exhale/inhale; signs for "Strokes a reed away from you" and "toward you" ("not always denoted"); a sign for an accent; "+" for a closed sound; "tr - trill"; "ha" and "ah" for abrupt start and stop; "*" for the pinched lamella; "Ta, Ka, Ti ... - Using syllables." — [Markov, Jew's Harp notation system](https://varganist.ru/491e.html)
- `[DESCRIBED]` Number tabs "created in 2011 and showed on IJHS congress in Yakutia", used "among Russian jew's harp players": digits for scale steps, "+" closed, "()" legato, "-" rest, "|:" ":|" repeats; example with rhythm "|5-(43)5-(43)3-(45)3---:|". — [Markov, Jew's Harp notation system](https://varganist.ru/491e.html)

**Tempo**
- `[DESCRIBED]` The only tempo figures found: a forum beginner's "tempo 50 in 4/4" (hardest) and "tempo 120 in 4/4" (easier), and Harman's "as many as 1,000 times per minute" for bursts (both in section 1). — [oberton.pro forum, "Learning the fast stroke"](https://www.oberton.pro/forum/topic/2721-%D0%BD%D0%B0%D1%83%D1%87%D0%B8%D1%82%D1%8C%D1%81%D1%8F-%D0%B1%D1%8B%D1%81%D1%82%D1%80%D0%BE%D0%BC%D1%83-%D0%B1%D0%BE%D1%8E/); [Harman, Mouthing Off #4](http://www.jewsharpguild.org/whmo4.html)

### Inferences
- (Mine.) Markov's notation is in effect a list of the independent control channels a player has per note: overtone (vowel), open/closed, breath direction, stroke direction, accent, glottal start, glottal stop, finger damping, trill, syllable, and tie (no new pluck). It is the best available checklist for the per-hit parameters of a synthesizer.
- (Mine.) The tie is the written form of the A/B split: an untied note = a pluck (class B event in the sequence); everything under a tie = articulation inside one pluck (class A).
- (Mine.) The beginner's remark that 50 bpm is harder than 120 bpm, and the teachers' insistence on evenness, suggest that typical rhythmic playing sits where one pluck per beat subdivision is comfortable; no source supports a specific range.

### Gaps
- No tempo ranges for any style from a teaching or research source; no bpm for morsing, khomus, munnharpe dance tunes or contemporary pieces.
- Not obtained: the lesson list of Jonny Cope's courses (a retailer article says "35 lessons" (snippet): [danmoi.com: online workshops](https://www.danmoi.com/where-can-i-find-good-online-workshops-to-learn-to-play-the-jews-harp); Udemy lists "6 sections, 35 lectures, 2h 33m" (snippet): [Udemy: Play the Jews Harp](https://www.udemy.com/course/play-the-jews-harp/)); the content of Janscha's and the Mayr brothers' books; Konchev's Altai school; the Markov and Bogatyreva tutor (its existence is attested only by a forum thread title); Robert Zagretdinov's kubyz school; any article of the Journal of the International Jew's Harp Society (the society's site announces "IJHS Journal #8": [jewsharpsociety.org](https://jewsharpsociety.org/)).
- No actual solkattu-to-morsing mapping (which syllable is played how) was found.

## 8. What marks the contemporary "trance / techno / beatbox" jaw harp style

### Takeaway
Text sources on this style are thin and mostly promotional or journalistic: they establish that players and bands present the acoustic jaw harp as dance music that "may sound familiar to even those who like electronic music", and that listeners compare it to an acid bass line, but they do not describe technique. - (Mine.) This is my synthesis; no source states it as a definition.
- (Mine.) The "acoustic TB-303" comparison points at what a rhythm-oriented synth must do well: a fixed-pitch drone source with a strongly resonant, fast-moving formant filter and per-step accent, which is exactly the per-hit parameter set listed in section 7.

## 9. For every entry: single-hit event (A) or sequence effect (B), and how long it lasts

### Takeaway
The sources themselves draw the line between what a pluck does and what happens while it rings: the notation tie (one finger beat, the rest "with the help of your speech apparatus and breath"), exercises counted "on one stroke", and the genggong's two layers (note stream versus accent pattern). Durations are almost never stated; the catalogue below is therefore my sorting, with the few sourced time facts attached.

### Cited Findings
- `[DESCRIBED]` The split in a teacher's own words: under a tie "the finger beat should be made only at the first note of all the tied notes, the rest of notes are produced with the help of your speech apparatus and breath"; "The rhythm is given by finger beats on the reed at each of not tied notes." — [Markov, Jew's Harp notation system](https://varganist.ru/491e.html)
- `[DESCRIBED]` "The pluck sets the tempo of your music. Maintaining a steady beat is critical." — [Harman, Mouthing Off #2](http://www.jewsharpguild.org/whmo2.html)
- `[OBSERVED]` *Syyia tardy*: "silent mouth articulations as though speak out syllables while the plucked lamella keeps vibrating." — [Nikolsky et al. 2020, footnote 16](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.03051/full)
- `[DESCRIBED]` Counted "on one stroke": several half-smiles; several lip closings; a pair of vowels; "three short and sharp inhales and exhales"; "two exhales". Counted across strokes: one breath per stroke; "one inhale for several strokes and one exhale for as many strokes". — [Aronov, Nizhny Novgorod vargan school](http://web.archive.org/web/20200225231341/http://www.vargan.ru:80/varganzal/varganschools/94-uprazhneniya-s-varganom-nizhegorodskaya-vargannaya-shkola)

Further inferences (mine):
- The engine-side list is longer than the note-side list. Almost everything that gives the instrument its "words" is class A, which fits the brief's premise that one MIDI note is one hit: the hit must carry a small articulation program (vowel target, glide, gate count, breath pulses, open/closed, damping, stop).
- The interactions between successive hits that the engine cannot ignore are few: (1) a new pluck interrupts the previous ring (choke/voice-steal behaviour, with whatever contact noise is right); (2) breath direction alternates and colours notes; (3) voice and long pharynx or lip swells may span several hits.
- Since no source gives sweep or gate times, the default durations should be set by ear against recordings and exposed as parameters, ideally tempo-synced, because players lock these gestures to the pulse (Aronov's counting, Vandré's "within this basic rhythm").

### Gaps
- The single-hit versus sequence sorting is not stated as such in any source beyond the notation tie and the "on one stroke" wording; the table is my reading and should be checked against recordings.
- No duration data at all for class A gestures; this cannot be closed from text sources. A measurement pass over recordings (spectrogram timing of formant sweeps, tap rates, breath pulse rates, inter-pluck intervals) is the only way to get milliseconds.

Scope: the instrument itself (lamella, frame, the gaps between them) as a source of sound, before and apart from what the mouth does to it. The vocal tract appears only where a source ties it to the source mechanism.

## 1. The lamella as a clamped-free bar: modes, fundamental frequency, tuning

### Takeaway
The lamella is a tapered, hand-shaped steel (or brass, bamboo) cantilever with a bent trigger at its tip; its first bending mode, typically 50–100 Hz on European forged instruments and 33–130 Hz across instruments on sale today, sets the pitch of everything that is heard. No source I could read publishes measured higher-mode frequencies of a jaw harp lamella; the nearest measurement is a plucked accordion reed (modes at 355 and 1645 Hz, ratio 1 : 4.6 against 1 : 6.27 for a uniform bar), and Leipp reports torsional vibration when the pluck is off the plane of the bend.

### Cited Findings

Fundamental frequencies
- **MEASURED.** "la guimbarde produit une large série de partiels quasi harmoniques du fondamental du système, qui varie généralement entre 50 et 100 Hz" [the guimbarde produces a wide series of quasi-harmonic partials of the system's fundamental, which generally lies between 50 and 100 Hz] (p. 13). The three Austrian instruments of his Figure 6 are labelled "guimbarde aigüe, fondamental ~80 Hz", "médium ~60 Hz", "grave ~50 Hz" (p. 12). — [Leipp 1967](https://www.lam.jussieu.fr/Publications/BulletinsGAM/GAM_25-La-Guimbarde.pdf)
- **MODEL / INTERPRETATION.** The pair of harps that Klier recommends for diatonic playing "ont comme fondamentaux respectivement 65 et 50 Hz environ et les premières notes utilisables sont les partiels 4" [have fundamentals of about 65 and 50 Hz, the first usable notes being partial 4] (p. 17, "un calcul élémentaire"). — [Leipp 1967](https://www.lam.jussieu.fr/Publications/BulletinsGAM/GAM_25-La-Guimbarde.pdf)
- **CLAIMED (vendor measurement table).** 130 models listed with average fundamental per model: minimum 33 Hz ("Uryung-Bas" Megakhomus), maximum 130 Hz ("Kurdyum" vargan), median 70 Hz, 10th–90th percentile 58–103 Hz (statistics computed by me from the table). — [Oberton Pro, Jaw harp measurements](https://www.oberton.pro/en/lab.htm?sort=frequency_avg)
- **DESCRIBED.** "The reed of every harp has its specific fundamental frequency, e.g. 98 Hz (tone G) or 58.3 Hz (B1)"; "a harp tuned A1 plays the multiplication tables from 55 Hz". — [Vandré, Tones and Pitches](http://www.rvandre.de/tones.html)
- **MEASURED (student).** Bamboo kubing, instrument length 24.5 cm, tongue length 11 cm: "The fundamental frequency of the kumbing (without the oral cavity to affect the harmonic series) is at about 70 Hz". — [UBC Phys341, Kumbing](https://wiki.ubc.ca/Course:Phys341_2020/Kumbing)
- **MEASURED (played at the mouth).** Yakut khomus, vowel [a]: h2 lies between 141 and 151 Hz. — [Nikolsky et al. 2020, Figure 3](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.03051/pdf)
- **CLAIMED (vendor).** Two Glazyrin models: base frequency 55–65 Hz and 65–75 Hz (snippet). — [Amazon listing, Glazyrin "Rainbow"](https://www.amazon.com/Jews-Harp-D-Glazyrin-Rainbow-Professional/dp/B01A71KE7K)
- **CLAIMED (museum catalogue).** Tongue pitches ("Zungenton") with dimensions: Molln 1950s, frame length 6.4 cm, tongue tone "es"; Austria 19th century, 5.04 cm, "h"; modern pair, 7.4 cm "Es" and 11.1 cm "F1" (the page prints "11,1 mm", evidently cm); Afghan čang, tongue 9.7 cm to the bend, 4.9 cm from the solder point to the bend, plucking part 1.7 cm, tone "b"; đàn môi pair, brass about 0.5 mm thick, tongue lengths 4.95 and 3.6 cm, tongue width 4 mm at the root, tones "E" and "H"; four-leaf kouxian, brass about 0.2 mm thick, tongue lengths 3.1, 2.4, 3.0, 2.5 cm, tones e1, h1, fis1, a1; Cambodian bamboo harps, tongue 9.7–9.8 cm, tones "F" and "ca. D"; Maranao kubing, 24.5–24.7 cm long, tone "e". — [Universität Würzburg, Maultrommeln](https://www.musikwissenschaft.uni-wuerzburg.de/musikinstrumente/bestand/inventarliste/r1-2-lo3-7-w3-7-maultrommeln/)
- **CLAIMED (collector's catalogue).** A German trump in A by Friedrich Schlütter: frame length 54.5 mm, overall width 38.5 mm, arm length 31 mm, "reed W 4.5 at slot, evenly tapering". — [Montagu, Trumps](https://www.jeremymontagu.co.uk/Trumps%20(Jews%20Harps)%20-%20Jeremy%20Montagu.epub)

How the lamella is made and how the fundamental is set
- **DESCRIBED (from the Molln maker Karl Schwarz).** The frame ("lyre") is bent from square iron wire "de 4 mm de côté environ", total length "entre 3 et 13 cm"; the reed is cut from steel plate, forged by hand "en aplatissant plus ou moins l'extrémité qui sera fixée sur le cadre", filed in places "indiqués par l'expérience", its narrow end bent at a right angle, then hardened; it is crimped into a notch in the frame and "on veille à ce que la languette passe au plus juste entre les branches" [care is taken that the reed passes as closely as possible between the arms] (pp. 6–8). — [Leipp 1967](https://www.lam.jussieu.fr/Publications/BulletinsGAM/GAM_25-La-Guimbarde.pdf)
- **MEASURED.** "nous avons vérifié, qu'une surcharge rapportée au bout libre de la lame, baisse plus ou moins le fondamental et toute la série de partiels, selon sa masse" [we verified that a load added at the free end lowers the fundamental and the whole series of partials, according to its mass] (p. 13). — [Leipp 1967](https://www.lam.jussieu.fr/Publications/BulletinsGAM/GAM_25-La-Guimbarde.pdf)
- **DESCRIBED.** "on peut 'accorder' l'instrument à volonté en collant à l'extrémité libre de la languette une boulette de cire ou de cire à cacheter" [the instrument can be tuned at will by sticking a pellet of wax or sealing wax on the free end of the reed] (p. 9). — [Leipp 1967](https://www.lam.jussieu.fr/Publications/BulletinsGAM/GAM_25-La-Guimbarde.pdf)
- **CLAIMED (maker).** Three ways to tune: (a) mass: "put a drop of tin on the tip of the tongue or in the corner of the knee and gently grind it down"; (b) effective length: "changing the angle of the knee" by "a few degrees"; (c) "reduce the stiffness of the reed by sharpening it as close to the base as possible - it will become softer, slower, the frequency will decrease". Instruments are sold as tuned when the fundamental is within ±10 cents of a note at A = 440 Hz. — [Oberton Pro FAQ](https://www.oberton.pro/en/info/faq.htm)
- **CLAIMED (museum).** Đàn môi are tuned by filing: removing material at the free end makes the tongue vibrate faster, removing it near the root makes it slower ("Wird in der Nähe der Wurzel ... Material abgetragen, wird die Schwingung langsamer"). — [Universität Würzburg](https://www.musikwissenschaft.uni-wuerzburg.de/musikinstrumente/bestand/inventarliste/r1-2-lo3-7-w3-7-maultrommeln/)
- **CLAIMED (encyclopedia).** "Výšku základního tónu udává délka a hmotnost jazýčku, může však být měněna v širokém rozsahu závažíčkem na jeho konci. Tím může být i hluboce a barvitě znějící brumle malá." [The pitch of the fundamental is given by the length and mass of the tongue, but it can be changed over a wide range by a small weight on its end; so even a deep-sounding harp can be small.] It can also be fine-tuned "změnou úhlu ohybu v jeho kolínku" [by changing the angle of the bend at its knee]. — [cs.wikipedia, Brumle](https://cs.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brumle)
- **CLAIMED (encyclopedia).** "Die Tonhöhe des Grundtons wird in erster Linie bestimmt durch die Länge, Dicke, Härte und Form der Feder. Die Breite der Feder wirkt sich in erster Linie auf Torsionsschwingungen aus." [The pitch is set mainly by the length, thickness, hardness and shape of the spring; its width mainly affects torsional vibrations.] — [de.wikipedia, Maultrommel](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maultrommel)
- **CLAIMED (vendor table).** Reed stiffness, defined as "the force needed to deflect the tip of the reed by 10 mm": median 92.5 gf, 10th–90th percentile 50–150 gf, maximum 450 gf. "Reed aspect ratio" (main reed length divided by the length of the bent end): 2.7–8.0, median 4.2 (statistics mine). — [Oberton Pro table](https://www.oberton.pro/en/lab.htm?sort=frequency_avg), definitions in the [FAQ](https://www.oberton.pro/en/info/faq.htm)
- **DESCRIBED.** Touching the vibrating reed briefly with a fingernail raises the pitch for an instant ("mordent"); pinching the reed between thumb and finger gives a muted tone of higher pitch ("finger capodastro"). — [Vandré, Playing techniques](http://www.rvandre.de/playtech.html)

Bar modes: theory and the nearest measurements
- **Textbook.** "Ein einseitig eingespannter, homogener Stab mit konstantem Querschnitt besitzt Eigenfrequenzen, die folgende Verhältnisreihe bilden: 1 : 6,27 : 17,57 : 34,37 : 56,84 ... Die Eigenfrequenzen bilden also keine harmonische Reihe." [A uniform clamped-free bar has natural frequencies in these ratios; they are not a harmonic series.] A reed plucked at its free end excites all modes, because every mode has an antinode there. — [Ziegenhals 2009](https://www.ifm-zwota.de/tonzun09.pdf)
- **MEASURED (laser vibrometer).** An accordion reed f1, plucked with a metal strip while mounted on its reed plate: the vibration spectrum shows "zwei deutliche Peaks 355 Hz und 1645 Hz. Sie bilden ein Frequenzverhältnis von 1 : 4,6", identified as the first two bending modes, "wobei das Frequenzverhältnis durch die Profilierung der Zunge gegenüber dem idealen Stab verschoben ist" [the ratio is shifted from the ideal bar by the profiling of the reed]. Further components are "sehr schwach und zudem deutlich bedämpft" [very weak and clearly damped]. — [Ziegenhals 2009](https://www.ifm-zwota.de/tonzun09.pdf)
- **MEASURED.** The displacement profile of a blown reed-organ reed at maximum amplitude fits Rayleigh's first cantilever mode ("the fit is quite good"); above normal playing pressure "some indications of the presence of the second beam mode (f2 = 6.27 f1) are observed in the spectrum of reed vibration" (equation restored by me from damaged PDF text, which reads "cf,= 6.27f,"). — [Cottingham, Lilly, Reed](https://www.cs.bu.edu/fac/snyder/TablatureWeb/BERLIN1.PDF)
- **MEASURED (abstract only).** In the starting transient of blown reed-organ reeds "the second transverse mode and the first torsional mode are both observed in the early stages of the transient, with the torsional mode often appearing earlier", although "the fundamental transverse mode is dominant". — [Cottingham and Wolff 2015](https://viennatalk2015.mdw.ac.at/abstracts/ViennaTalk2015_submission_39.pdf); same finding in [Cottingham 2017](https://isma2017.cirmmt.mcgill.ca/proceedings/pdf/ISMA_2017_paper_52.pdf)
- **MODEL / INTERPRETATION.** "Les équations des verges libres-encastrées sont bien connues et montrent que de tels systèmes ne peuvent donner des séries d'harmoniques au sens rigoureux du terme. En fait, il ne s'agit pas ici d'une verge théorique, mais d'un système complexe" [the clamped-free rod equations show that such systems cannot give harmonic series in the strict sense; but this is not a theoretical rod, it is a complex system] (p. 12). — [Leipp 1967](https://www.lam.jussieu.fr/Publications/BulletinsGAM/GAM_25-La-Guimbarde.pdf)
- **MODEL / INTERPRETATION.** Torsion: successive strokes differ because "on ne pince pas la lame exactement chaque fois dans le plan du coude. Ceci occasionne des vibrations de torsion d'autant plus compliquées que la forme géométrique de la languette n'est pas simple du fait du martelage irrégulier" [the blade is not plucked exactly in the plane of the bend each time; this causes torsional vibrations, complicated by the irregular hammered shape] (p. 13). — [Leipp 1967](https://www.lam.jussieu.fr/Publications/BulletinsGAM/GAM_25-La-Guimbarde.pdf)
- **CLAIMED (maker).** "a shorter bent end tends to produce fewer unwanted vibrations when plucked away from the face, making fast playing more comfortable and the sound clearer". — [Oberton Pro FAQ](https://www.oberton.pro/en/info/faq.htm)
- **CLAIMED (as reported).** For the Balinese genggong (idioglot, cut from the leaf stem of a sugar palm), Rai 2004 as quoted by Morgan: "the lamella by itself produced the one kind of sound quality, with complex non-harmonic partials, and thus without clearly defined pitch". — [Morgan 2008, ch. 5](https://facultystaff.richmond.edu/~amcgraw/gamelan/ubc_2008_fall_morgan_deirdre-libre.pdf)
- **CLAIMED (encyclopedia citing Leipp 1963, which I did not read).** "La languette de la guimbarde est, du point de vue mécanique, une lame vibrante, sans résonateur, sous de nombreux modes, avec une décroissance assez rapide, qui peut être de 20 décibels par seconde, et des partiels non harmoniques à des fréquences jusqu'à quelques centaines de hertz." [Mechanically the reed is a vibrating blade without a resonator, with many modes, a fairly fast decay that can be 20 dB per second, and non-harmonic partials at frequencies up to a few hundred hertz.] — [fr.wikipedia, Guimbarde](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guimbarde_(instrument))

### Inferences
- Mode ratios of a uniform clamped-free bar, computed by me from cos λ · cosh λ = −1 (λ = 1.8751, 4.6941, 7.8548, 10.9955): 1 : 6.267 : 17.547 : 34.386. This matches the 1 : 6.27 : 17.55 of the brief; Ziegenhals prints 17,57 and 34,37.
- First-mode frequency of a uniform rectangular cantilever, f1 = 0.1615 · (h / L²) · √(E/ρ) (standard Euler–Bernoulli result, written from memory, not from a fetched source). With steel (√(E/ρ) ≈ 5050 m/s): L = 70 mm, h = 0.5 mm gives 83 Hz; L = 70 mm, h = 0.4 mm gives 67 Hz; L = 60 mm, h = 0.5 mm gives 113 Hz. Real lamellae are tapered, thinned near the root and carry the bent trigger, so this is an order-of-magnitude check only: it shows that 0.4–0.6 mm steel of 60–80 mm free length lands in the reported 50–100 Hz range.
- Effect of a point mass at the tip (my numerical solution of the standard frequency equation 1 + cos λ cosh λ + μλ(cos λ sinh λ − sin λ cosh λ) = 0, with μ = tip mass / bar mass; rotary inertia of the hook ignored):

| μ | f1 relative to bare bar | f2/f1 | f3/f1 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 1.000 | 6.27 | 17.55 |
| 0.05 | 0.912 | 6.35 | 17.94 |
| 0.1 | 0.844 | 6.52 | 18.71 |
| 0.2 | 0.743 | 6.97 | 20.50 |
| 0.5 | 0.573 | 8.38 | 25.64 |
| 1 | 0.443 | 10.43 | 32.68 |

  So a hook, a blob of solder or wax lowers f1 strongly and pushes the higher bending modes further away from it (ratios rise). Profiling works the other way in Ziegenhals's accordion reed (ratio 4.6). For a jaw harp of 70 Hz the second bending mode would then sit somewhere between about 320 Hz (ratio 4.6) and 600 Hz or more (ratio 8 and above); this range is my estimate, not a measurement.
- This also gives a test of what the "partials" are. If the radiated partials were bar modes, a tip load would move them by different factors (f1 falls 43 % at μ = 0.5 while f2/f1 rises from 6.27 to 8.38). Leipp measured that the load lowers "le fondamental et toute la série de partiels" together, which is what harmonics locked to the fundamental do.
- From the vendor's median stiffness (92.5 gf per 10 mm ≈ 91 N/m at the tip) and 70 Hz, the effective moving mass is k/(2πf)² ≈ 0.47 g, and the energy stored by a 5 mm tip deflection is about 1.1 mJ (10 mm: 4.5 mJ). Linear-spring arithmetic of mine.
- If the Würzburg tongue pitches follow Helmholtz notation (my assumption: "es" = E♭3 ≈ 156 Hz, "h" = B3 ≈ 247 Hz, "Es" = E♭2 ≈ 78 Hz, "F1" = contra F ≈ 44 Hz, "e1" = E4 ≈ 330 Hz), small old Austrian harps (5–6.4 cm) reach 150–250 Hz and brass leaf harps (kouxian) several hundred Hz, well above Leipp's "50 to 100 Hz". The uniform-bar formula gives only about 115 Hz for a 31 mm × 0.2 mm brass strip (taking √(E/ρ) ≈ 3400 m/s for brass), so for those pointed, filed tongues the shape matters a great deal (or my reading of the octave is wrong).

### Gaps
- No modal measurement of a jaw harp lamella (laser vibrometer, high-speed video analysis, impact test) was found: no measured f2/f1, no torsional frequency, no measured effect of the hook. The Würzburg page links a high-speed video of a đàn môi by Mühlhans, Esper and Reuter, University of Vienna (https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4jM6wG4aOI8); I could not view it and found no accompanying paper.
- Whether the second bending mode or the torsional mode is audible in the radiated sound of a jaw harp is not answered by any measurement I could read. The evidence is indirect: Leipp's torsion remark, Ziegenhals's plucked accordion reed (section 3), the genggong remark, and fr.wikipedia's summary of Leipp 1963, which I could not check against the original.
- Leipp 1963 (Acustica), the fullest acoustic study of the instrument, was not obtained.

## 2. What makes the sound: direct radiation of the lamella, or modulation of air through the gaps

### Takeaway
The researchers who examined the question (Crane 1968, Ledang 1972, Adkins 1974, and Ziegenhals 2009 for the plucked accordion reed) agree that the heard spectrum is not radiated by the bar as such: it is generated when the lamella passes through the narrow slot between the frame arms and produces short pressure pulses, the lamella acting as the timing oscillator and the slot as the valve. Ledang's experiments put the largest useful clearance at 0.2 mm; makers work at 10–200 µm and all say that a smaller gap gives a louder sound with a wider spectrum. Leipp's 1967 bulletin does not describe slot pulses and treats lamella, frame and teeth as one coupled mechanical exciter (the abstract of his 1963 paper, seen only as a snippet, does call the blade a "pulsating exciter" of the mouth cavity). The full texts of Crane, Ledang and Adkins were not available, so their experimental detail is known here only through abstracts and summaries.

### Cited Findings

Older theories, as reviewed by Leipp
- **CLAIMED (historical review).** Mersenne asked whether the reed follows the string law or the pendulum law; Praetorius called it a "mixed" instrument between percussion and winds; Savart "pense qu'il s'agit d'un instrument à anche vibrante, laissant croire qu'il fonctionne à l'aide de 'vent', ce qui est manifestement inexact ou du moins insuffisant" [thinks it is a vibrating-reed instrument working with wind, which is plainly inexact or at least insufficient]; Wheatstone, Weber and Sachs hold that the mouth amplifies the "harmonics" of the reed, "le 16° 'harmonique' serait encore perceptible"; Bouasse holds that the instrument is only "un moyen pratique pour donner des chocs sur les dents", the teeth then exciting the mouth cavity as a continuously tunable resonator (pp. 10–11). — [Leipp 1967](https://www.lam.jussieu.fr/Publications/BulletinsGAM/GAM_25-La-Guimbarde.pdf)
- **CLAIMED (Grove 1880, condensing Wheatstone 1828).** "The vibration of the tongue itself corresponds with a very low sound; but the cavity of the mouth is capable of various alterations; and when the number of vibrations of the contained volume of air is any multiple of the original vibrations of the tongue, a sound is produced corresponding to the modification of the oral cavity." — [Grove 1880, reprint](https://musikwissenschaften.de/lexikon/m/maultrommel/jews-harp-1880/)

Crane 1968 (not read; as reported)
- **MODEL / INTERPRETATION, as reported by Kolltveit.** "Frederick Crane claimed that the jew's harp functions only when a stream of air passes the embouchure portion of the instrument. Ledang 1972 (abstract read; results as reported)
- **Abstract.** "Numerous theories of the sound producing mechanism of the jaw's harp have been put forward, none of which appears quite convincing or appropriate ... - **MEASURED, as reported by Kolltveit (citing Ledang 1972, pp. 97–101).** "He noticed that makers and players of jew's harps know that the arms of the frame are as important as the lamella, and that the distance between the inner edges of the arms and the lamella should be as small as possible in order to make the instrument sound well. He concluded that the turbulence in the air surrounding the lamella generates an essential harmonic spectrum required for playing". — [Kolltveit 2016](https://musark.no/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/J-Harps-of-Bone-Wood-and-Metal.Kolltveit-2016.pdf)

Additionally, the smaller the distance between lamella and frame, the richer the harmonic spectrum and the longer it is sustained." Footnote: "Both Crane and Ledang made similar observations and reached similar conclusions independently of each other." — [Morgan 2008, pp. 22–23](https://facultystaff.richmond.edu/~amcgraw/gamelan/ubc_2008_fall_morgan_deirdre-libre.pdf)

Adkins 1974 (abstract read; rest as reported)
- **Abstract, verbatim.** "It is shown that the jew's harp excites the mouth cavity by producing short pulses when the lamella passes through the frame. A model is proposed for the mechanism by which the pulses are generated. When the mouth cavity is tuned to low frequencies, its natural resonance time is longer than the period between successive pulses and the harmonic series of the fundamental frequency of the lamella dominates the sound as the mouth tuning is changed. At high frequencies, the mouth cavity and ear respond to each successive pulse independently so that the dominant sensation of pitch coincides with the natural resonance frequency of the mouth and may be tuned continuously." Author affiliation: Royal Society Mond Laboratory, Cambridge. — [Adkins 1974, Crossref record](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1121/1.1914580), DOI https://doi.org/10.1121/1.1914580
- **As reported (conditions of the experiment).** Adkins worked on a Balinese genggong "in conditions far-removed from those of performance: the instrument was held in a vice grip and its lamella was set into motion without any sort of resonator to amplify its overtones". — [Morgan 2008, p. 24](https://facultystaff.richmond.edu/~amcgraw/gamelan/ubc_2008_fall_morgan_deirdre-libre.pdf)
- **MODEL, as reported (student's paraphrase of Adkins's mechanism).** "When the tongue moves through the frame, the airflow around the tongue is relatively free. This sudden change increases the air pressure in front and decreases the air pressure behind. When the tongue clears the parallel arms, the pressure balances out in front and behind the tongue, causing air to flow backwards." — [Li 2015](https://courses.physics.illinois.edu/phys406/sp2017/Student_Projects/Spring15/Simon_Li_Physics_406_Final_Report_Sp15.pdf)

- **CLAIMED.** "Plucked on its own, the instrument can only produce a single note"; it "requires a resonator to hear any of these harmonics in isolation". — [Morgan 2008, p. 24](https://facultystaff.richmond.edu/~amcgraw/gamelan/ubc_2008_fall_morgan_deirdre-libre.pdf)
- **MEASURED (player's own).** A harp fixed in a vice and plucked gives a spectrum with peaks at the same frequencies as when it is played at the mouth; the mouth only raises the peaks in its formant regions ("the jew's harp has the same tones as played without contact to the mouth ... the peaks of both curves are at the same frequencies"). — [Vandré, Tones and Pitches](http://www.rvandre.de/tones.html)

- **CLAIMED (student).** "It is a known fact that the jaw harp cannot produce a sound (even in a cavity) unless the frame is touching a solid object (like your teeth)." — [Li 2015](https://courses.physics.illinois.edu/phys406/sp2017/Student_Projects/Spring15/Simon_Li_Physics_406_Final_Report_Sp15.pdf)
- **CLAIMED (maker).** Closing the lips on the frame arms makes the sound louder ("start closing up the lips slowly on the frame to make the produced sound louder"). — [Oberton Pro, handling](https://www.oberton.pro/en/info/handling/)
- **MEASURED (free reed, abstract only).** For a blown accordion reed, "the sound is found to be dominated by the dipolar source associated by the strong momentum fluctuations of the flow through the reed gaps". — [Ricot, Caussé, Misdariis 2005](https://doi.org/10.1121/1.1852546)

### Inferences
- The sources fit one picture: the lamella is a lightly damped oscillator that fixes the timing (its first bending mode), and the slot is a valve that turns each passage of the lamella into a short pressure and flow pulse. The direct radiation of a strip a few millimetres wide moving at 50–100 Hz is not what carries the many partials. Ziegenhals says this outright for the blown accordion reed ("der durch die schwingende Zunge abgestrahlte Schall ohne Bedeutung" [the sound radiated by the vibrating reed itself is of no importance]), and for the jaw harp it follows from Ledang's gap experiment and Adkins's pulses.
- Leipp 1967 and the pulse school disagree on the cause of the quasi-harmonic series (coupled mechanical system against slot pulses), not on the observation. Leipp's two measurements (many partials radiated even away from the mouth; the whole series following the fundamental when a load is added) are both what the pulse mechanism predicts.
- Ledang's "not exceeding 0.2 millimetres" and the vendor's 10–200 µm agree. A synthesis parameter "gap" would therefore span roughly 0.01–0.2 mm, with smaller meaning louder, brighter and longer-sounding.
- Edge-on square bar means the lamella is closely enclosed only over a short distance of its travel. That favours short pulses; no source measures the pulse duration.

### Gaps
- The full texts of Crane 1968, Ledang 1972 and Adkins 1974 were not available. Unknown as a result: Ledang's instruments, spectrograph settings and the gap values tested; Adkins's oscillograms, the pulse shape and duration, his pulse-generation model, and whether he measured without a mouth only.
- Dournon-Taurelle and Wright 1978 could not be obtained; its existence and typology are confirmed through Kolltveit only, and I cannot say what it states on acoustics.
- No modern flow visualization, pressure measurement at the slot, or aeroacoustic simulation of a jaw harp was found before the search budget ran out.
- Nothing was found on what is heard with the instrument against closed lips.
- "Turbulence" is the word used by Ledang's summarizers; Adkins's abstract speaks of pulses. Whether Ledang meant turbulence in the fluid-dynamic sense cannot be settled without his text.

## 3. The source waveform and spectrum

### Takeaway
The radiated signal of the bare instrument is a line spectrum on the lamella's fundamental with a very large number of components (Leipp: up to 60 partials when plucked in front of a microphone; Nikolsky: 145 harmonics above the noise floor at the mouth), in which the odd-numbered harmonics dominate and the even ones, h2 in particular, are weak or missing (Leipp, Vandré, Li, Nikolsky; one student page says the opposite). Adkins attributes the sound to short pulses produced each time the lamella passes through the frame; a maker states that the lamella passes the edges in both directions with equal strength when it rests in the frame plane and unequally when it is offset. The spectrum is not smoothly falling: Leipp reports formant-like regions due to frame and teeth and stroke-to-stroke differences.

### Cited Findings

Pulses
- **MODEL / INTERPRETATION (abstract only).** The jew's harp "excites the mouth cavity by producing short pulses when the lamella passes through the frame"; he speaks of "the period between successive pulses". — [Adkins 1974](https://doi.org/10.1121/1.1914580)
- **CLAIMED (maker).** "The tongue moves over the decks in two directions, and in case its state of rest is within the frame plane, moving into both sides takes one and the same time, and the force of each air wave generated is the same, this mode generates smooth, classical jew's harp sound. In case the tongue is displaced, the oscillations are divided into two – even and odd ones; they come one by one with different forces." Also: "Sound loudness depends on the speed of tongue movement against the frame edges", the tip moving fastest in the middle of its arc; and off the middle line "the tongue is bent, and the bigger the bend angle, the more curved the tongue line, so the sound gets smeared time-wise and frequency-wise". — [Oberton Pro, Why a jew's harp should not buzz](https://www.oberton.pro/en/info/nobuzz/)
- **MEASURED (blown free reed, transferable).** "Although the reed motion appears to be approximately sinusoidal, the sound pressure waveform somewhat resembles a pulse wave, as the reed acts as a valve, alternately opening and closing the opening in the reed frame twice in each cycle of oscillation." — [Cottingham, Lilly, Reed](https://www.cs.bu.edu/fac/snyder/TablatureWeb/BERLIN1.PDF)
- **MEASURED / MODEL (blown free reed).** "the airflow rate occurs in two puffs per cycle, each puff corresponding to the reed opening on one or the other side of the reed plate"; "The smaller puffs correspond to the reed passing below the plate"; the pressure waveform near an accordion reed is an "approximate square wave ... similar to that obtained from a siren" and "the sound spectrum has abundant higher harmonics". — [Cottingham 2011](https://physicstoday.aip.org/features/acoustics-of-free-reed-instruments)
- **MODEL / INTERPRETATION.** "Der durch die Zungenschwingung unterbrochene Luftstrom bildet eine Impulsfolge mit fester Grundfrequenz. Das Spektrum einer solchen Impulsfolge ist eine streng harmonische Reihe auf Basis der Grundfrequenz." [The air stream interrupted by the reed forms a pulse train of fixed fundamental frequency; the spectrum of such a train is a strictly harmonic series.] — [Ziegenhals 2009](https://www.ifm-zwota.de/tonzun09.pdf)

Which harmonics, how many
- **MEASURED.** Plucked in front of a microphone (not at the mouth): "une large série de partiels (jusqu'à 60....) dont les numéros impairs sont prédominants, et les pairs très faibles" [a wide series of partials, up to 60, the odd-numbered predominant and the even very weak] (p. 12, Figure 6: three sonagrams, frequency axis to 2.5 kHz, a 1-second time mark, partials 1, 3, 5, 7, 9 labelled). — [Leipp 1967](https://www.lam.jussieu.fr/Publications/BulletinsGAM/GAM_25-La-Guimbarde.pdf)
- **MEASURED.** "Ce spectre n'est pas régulièrement décroissant vers l'aigu, comme le voudrait la théorie élémentaire : on y remarque des régions formantiques privilégiées ... et les partiels impairs sont plus intenses que les pairs. Les coups successifs sur une même lame ont une allure similaire mais diffèrent souvent beaucoup de l'un à l'autre dans le détail: l'amplitude varie à chaque coup" [the spectrum does not fall regularly toward the treble as elementary theory would have it: there are preferred formant regions, and odd partials are stronger than even; successive strokes look similar but often differ a lot in detail, the amplitude varies at each stroke] (p. 13). — [Leipp 1967](https://www.lam.jussieu.fr/Publications/BulletinsGAM/GAM_25-La-Guimbarde.pdf)
- **MEASURED.** A tip load changes "les conditions de l'excitation, donc peu ou prou les spectres; mais ceux-ci conservent une allure générale très caractéristique" [the excitation and so more or less the spectra, which keep a very characteristic general shape] (p. 13). — [Leipp 1967](https://www.lam.jussieu.fr/Publications/BulletinsGAM/GAM_25-La-Guimbarde.pdf)
- **MEASURED (player's own, Audacity).** A harp with a 58.3 Hz fundamental recorded "without contact to the mouth": peaks "at regular intervalls of 58 Hz"; "Without resonance ... the peaks of odd-numbered overtones (no. 1, 3 and so on as counted from left to right) are lower than the even ones (no. 2, 4 and so on)". In harmonic numbering this means h2, h4, h6 are weaker than h3, h5, h7. A second harp, tuned G (98 Hz), was measured with "the frame ... fixed to a vice". — [Vandré, Tones and Pitches](http://www.rvandre.de/tones.html)
- **MEASURED (student; harps played at the mouth with a "normal" cavity).** For a tenor (F) and an alto (G) steel harp: "the odd harmonics dominate the spectrum, with another peak around 3000 Hz". — [Li 2015](https://courses.physics.illinois.edu/phys406/sp2017/Student_Projects/Spring15/Simon_Li_Physics_406_Final_Report_Sp15.pdf)
- **MEASURED (played at the mouth; 44.1 kHz, 16 bit, FFT of 262,144 samples, average of 4 takes).** Yakut khomus, vowel [a]: "There are 145 harmonics discernible above the noise floor, peaking at f9", against 23 "active" harmonics for bass singing and 19 for speech, both peaking at f1. "JH reserves higher intensity for odd harmonics, as opposed to speaking and singing." "The bandwidth of harmonics is significantly narrower for khomus than for singing and speaking": at the baseline, khomus h2 spans 141–151 Hz, "117 cents (a semitone)", against 349 cents for sung and 325 cents for spoken [a]; the khomus spectrum is also much less noisy. — [Nikolsky et al. 2020, Figure 3](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.03051/pdf)
- **MEASURED / CLAIMED.** "JHs made of organic materials usually deviate from the harmonic series in the tuning of partials. A JH can also omit a harmonic (usually f2), or generate a non-serial partial or a subharmonic". Across recordings of instruments of five materials, "In most cases, the texture spreads from about 70 Hz up to 3–5 kHz". — [Nikolsky et al. 2020](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.03051/pdf)
- **MEASURED (student; bamboo kubing).** "The second harmonic (~140 Hz) is missing and the following odd-numbered harmonics are relatively weaker than its even counterparts as it is similar to an closed-open tube." — [UBC Phys341, Kumbing](https://wiki.ubc.ca/Course:Phys341_2020/Kumbing). This contradicts Leipp, Vandré, Li and Nikolsky on which set is weaker, and is internally inconsistent (a missing h2 and a closed-open tube both mean weak even harmonics).
- **CLAIMED (snippet).** Closing the velum leaves mainly odd-numbered harmonics audible. — [Grove Music Online, Jew's harp](https://www.oxfordmusiconline.com/grovemusic/display/10.1093/gmo/9781561592630.001.0001/omo-9781561592630-e-0000014300)

Is the spectrum strictly harmonic
- **MEASURED / INTERPRETATION.** The system "fournit une série de partiels voisins de certains harmoniques du fondamental de la lame qu'on peut pratiquement assimiler à une série harmonique" [gives a series of partials close to certain harmonics of the reed's fundamental, which can in practice be treated as a harmonic series] (p. 12); "la fréquence fondamentale de la lame et tout le spectre de raies restent en principe fixes pendant toutes les expériences" [the fundamental and the whole line spectrum stay fixed in principle during all the experiments] whatever the mouth does (p. 15). — [Leipp 1967](https://www.lam.jussieu.fr/Publications/BulletinsGAM/GAM_25-La-Guimbarde.pdf)
- **MEASURED (plucked accordion reed in its plate, unblown).** The reed vibrates in two modes (355 and 1645 Hz); the radiated sound contains both, "Sie dominieren aber keineswegs das Spektrum" [but they by no means dominate the spectrum]. "Da die Zunge mit zwei Eigenschwingungen schwingt, muss es auch zwei Impulse und damit zwei harmonische Reihen geben. Tatsächlich findet man im Schallspektrum eine Reihe auf 355 Hz und eine auf 1645 Hz. Darüber hinaus existieren Frequenzkomponenten, die in keine der beiden Reihen passen. Deren Ursprung kann momentan nicht erklärt werden." [Since the reed vibrates in two modes there must be two pulses and two harmonic series; the sound spectrum indeed shows a series on 355 Hz and one on 1645 Hz, plus components that fit neither series and cannot be explained at present.] When the same reed is blown, the sound contains only the harmonic series on about 350 Hz; the second mode "ist stark bedämpft, aber durchaus vorhanden" in the reed's motion, but no sound components attributable to it are found. — [Ziegenhals 2009](https://www.ifm-zwota.de/tonzun09.pdf)
- **CLAIMED / as reported.** "while the basic overtones are those of the harmonic series, it is clear that non-harmonic pitches can also be obtained, particularly in the upper ranges" (Montagu, citing Adkins). Adkins's abstract gives the mechanism: at high frequencies the mouth "respond[s] to each successive pulse independently so that the dominant sensation of pitch coincides with the natural resonance frequency of the mouth and may be tuned continuously". — [Montagu](https://www.jeremymontagu.co.uk/Trumps%20(Jews%20Harps)%20-%20Jeremy%20Montagu.epub); [Adkins 1974](https://doi.org/10.1121/1.1914580)
- **CLAIMED (encyclopedia summarizing Leipp 1963).** The reed alone has "des partiels non harmoniques à des fréquences jusqu'à quelques centaines de hertz"; the mouth's acoustic impedance reacts on the reed's vibration, "changeant ses modes, et produisant des sons approximativement harmoniques". — [fr.wikipedia](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guimbarde_(instrument)). This differs from Leipp 1967, where the quasi-harmonic series is already there in front of a microphone, without a mouth.

Dependence on level, gap and breath
- **MEASURED (blown accordion reed).** The waveform changes shape with level, not just size: at piano the dominance of the signal parts produced while the reed is above the plate recedes, "was bedeutet, dass sich die Klangfarbe verändert" [which means that the timbre changes]. — [Ziegenhals 2009](https://www.ifm-zwota.de/tonzun09.pdf)
- **MEASURED (blown harmonium reed, laser vibrometer).** "at lower blowing pressures (≈ 0.2 kPa) the motion of the reed is largely sinusoidal while the higher harmonic components become more prominent at the higher blowing pressures (1.4-1.6 kPa)". — [Puranik and Scavone 2023](https://dael.euracoustics.org/confs/fa2023/data/articles/000853.pdf)
- **As reported / CLAIMED.** Smaller gap, richer and longer-sustained spectrum (Ledang via Morgan); wide gaps reduce loudness and spectral width (maker). — [Morgan 2008](https://facultystaff.richmond.edu/~amcgraw/gamelan/ubc_2008_fall_morgan_deirdre-libre.pdf); [Oberton Pro FAQ](https://www.oberton.pro/en/info/faq.htm)
- **MEASURED, damaged text.** With breath: "On vérifie que tout le spectre se modifie au lieu des harmoniques pairs, ce sont les impairs qui prédominent [avec le souffle; le] timbre change" (p. 16; the words in brackets are barely legible in the scan). As printed this says that with breath the odd harmonics predominate instead of the even, which conflicts with p. 12, where the odd ones already predominate without breath. Either "pairs" and "impairs" are swapped in the typescript or the sentence means something I cannot recover. What is safe: breath changes the odd/even balance of the whole spectrum. — [Leipp 1967](https://www.lam.jussieu.fr/Publications/BulletinsGAM/GAM_25-La-Guimbarde.pdf)
- **MEASURED (student).** Breathing out while playing: "the general spectrum has not changed"; holding the breath with the throat open to the lungs removed the peak near 3000 Hz. — [Li 2015](https://courses.physics.illinois.edu/phys406/sp2017/Student_Projects/Spring15/Simon_Li_Physics_406_Final_Report_Sp15.pdf)

### Inferences
- Number of components in hertz (my arithmetic): 60 partials on 50–80 Hz instruments reach 3.0–4.8 kHz; Nikolsky's khomus has h2 at 141–151 Hz, so h1 ≈ 73 Hz and 145 harmonics reach about 10.6 kHz.
- One or two pulses per cycle, and the odd/even question. The lamella crosses the frame plane twice per period, once in each direction. If each crossing gives a pulse and the two pulses have opposite sign (in Adkins's piston picture as paraphrased by Li, air is pushed ahead of the lamella, so the pressure change on a given side reverses with the direction of travel), then for a lamella resting in the frame plane the signal has half-wave antisymmetry and contains only odd harmonics. That matches four independent observations of weak even harmonics, two of them made away from the mouth (Leipp, Vandré). No source states the polarity argument; it is mine. Two equal pulses of the same sign, half a period apart, would instead give a spectrum on 2·f0 (even harmonics only), which no source reports. A blown free reed is different: the flow direction is fixed by the blowing pressure, so the two puffs per cycle have the same sign and unequal size, and all harmonics appear.
- Consequence of an offset rest position (my derivation, sinusoidal lamella motion of amplitude A, frame plane at distance d from the rest position, opposite-sign pulses of equal size): the two crossings are separated by T/2 − (T/π)·arcsin(d/A), and the amplitude of harmonic n is proportional to |cos(n·arcsin(d/A))| for odd n and |sin(n·arcsin(d/A))| for even n. For d/A = 0.05, h2 is at 0.10 and h4 at 0.20 of the odd-harmonic level; for d/A = 0.2, h2 is at 0.39 and h4 at 0.72. Since A decays while d stays fixed, d/A grows during every note: even harmonics should rise relative to odd ones as the note dies, and once A < d the lamella no longer reaches the frame plane and the pulses stop although the lamella is still moving. The maker's statement that a bent tongue shortens the fade-out and "divides" the oscillations into even and odd ones of different force describes the same thing qualitatively. A steady breath flow would break the antisymmetry in a similar way, which is one possible reading of Leipp's damaged sentence.
- Pulse width and brightness (my estimate). If a pulse lasts while the lamella travels a distance s through the enclosed zone, its duration is about s / (2π·f0·A) at the centre crossing. With f0 = 80 Hz, A = 5 mm the tip speed is 2.5 m/s; s = 1 mm gives 0.4 ms, a spectral envelope reaching about 2.5 kHz; at A = 1 mm the pulse lasts 2 ms and the envelope shrinks to about 0.5 kHz. So both the level and the bandwidth of the source should fall as the amplitude decays: the upper partials die first. Leipp's Figure 6 shows this (section 5), and it fits the maker's "loudness depends on the speed of tongue movement against the frame edges". The value of s is a guess; nobody has published it.
- Why the series is harmonic although bar modes are not: a periodic pulse train has a strictly harmonic line spectrum whatever the shape of the pulse (Ziegenhals). Inharmonic content then has three possible origins that sources name: a second pulse series locked to the second bending mode right after the pluck (Ziegenhals, plucked reed), torsion from an off-plane pluck (Leipp), and at high frequencies the mouth ringing at its own resonance between pulses (Adkins). The narrow 117-cent baseline width of the khomus harmonics shows how clean the lines are in sustained playing.
- The odd-harmonic dominance cannot be explained by the mouth alone (the Grove snippet and the UBC remark point to a closed tube), because Leipp and Vandré measured it without a mouth. Both effects may add when the glottis is closed.

### Gaps
- No published pressure waveform of an unblown, plucked jaw harp was found; Adkins's figures were not seen. The polarity of the two pulses, their relative size and their duration are therefore not documented.
- No numeric spectral slope (dB per octave) for the bare source was found. Leipp only says that it does not fall regularly.
- No source read measures the spectrum as a function of pluck amplitude or of gap width with numbers; Ledang's data would be the place to look.
- The UBC page's claim that odd harmonics are weaker stands alone and looks like a slip, but I cannot check its figures against raw data.

## 4. How strong the fundamental itself is in the radiated sound (the "drone")

### Takeaway
Sources agree that the drone is always heard, but the measurements show that the component at h1 is weak in the radiated sound: Leipp attributes the tanpura-like quality of the "pedal" to "the absence or weakness of the low partials", and in Nikolsky's khomus spectrum the strongest harmonic is h9, not h1. The drone that listeners hear is therefore carried largely by the evenly spaced higher lines.

### Cited Findings
- **MEASURED / INTERPRETATION.** "on perçoit nettement le réseau de raies fixe dont l'équidistance est d'environ 80 Hz dans le cas considéré; en d'autres termes il existe une 'pédale' grave invariable de 80 Hz" [one clearly perceives the fixed grid of lines spaced about 80 Hz; in other words there is an invariable low pedal of 80 Hz] (p. 15). "La pédale grave rappelle par certains aspects le son du tampoura ... dû à l'absence ou au peu d'intensité des partiels graves" [the low pedal recalls the tanpura in some respects, owing to the absence or weakness of the low partials] (p. 16). — [Leipp 1967](https://www.lam.jussieu.fr/Publications/BulletinsGAM/GAM_25-La-Guimbarde.pdf)
- **MEASURED.** Khomus [a]: the spectrum peaks at h9, while sung and spoken [a] peak at h1; "F1 in the JH is much softer than in speaking and singing. The JH's F2 is much stronger." The authors divide the spectrum into a "harmonic base" (h1–h4, up to h7) and a "harmonic residue", the border between the "instrumental" and "vocal" ranges lying at about h6–h8; the instrument's own "instrumental formant" falls on h5 in six of eight Yakut vowels and spreads over h3–h5 in the other two. — [Nikolsky et al. 2020](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.03051/pdf)
- **MODEL / INTERPRETATION.** "The sum of the unresolved partials that comprise 'pitch residue' is perceived as a pitch identical to FF, but harsher in timbre"; players contrast vowels "only in relation to the 'residual tone,' not the 'fundamental tone.'" — [Nikolsky et al. 2020](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.03051/pdf)
- **MEASURED (student; kubing with a 70 Hz fundamental, breath held).** For an 'o' mouth shape "the 'o' vowel's lowest frequency is at 210 Hz however faint with its loudest frequency at around 770 Hz". — [UBC Phys341, Kumbing](https://wiki.ubc.ca/Course:Phys341_2020/Kumbing)
- **MEASURED (player's own).** In the spectrum recorded without the mouth, the fundamental is the first peak, the second harmonic is "smaller", the third "higher". — [Vandré, Tones and Pitches](http://www.rvandre.de/tones.html)
- **DESCRIBED.** Plucking "produces the fundamental note (or prime tone) of the instrument that is heard continuously while playing"; "When breathing in and out, the air stream amplifies the fundamental note." — [Vandré, Playing techniques](http://www.rvandre.de/playtech.html)
- **DESCRIBED.** A beginner hears "subtle overtones hovering above its fundamental low-pitched buzzing frequency"; for the Balinese genggong "it is often difficult to perceive a definite pitch for the fundamental". — [Morgan 2008](https://facultystaff.richmond.edu/~amcgraw/gamelan/ubc_2008_fall_morgan_deirdre-libre.pdf)
- **CLAIMED.** "With the jew's harp the fundamental serves as a drone." — [Kolltveit 2006](https://musark.no/wp-content/uploads/2013/11/Thesis-intro.pdf). "... wobei die Grundfrequenz immer enthalten ist. Daher zählt die Maultrommel auch zu den Borduninstrumenten." [the fundamental frequency is always contained; hence the instrument also counts among the drone instruments] — [Universität Würzburg](https://www.musikwissenschaft.uni-wuerzburg.de/musikinstrumente/bestand/inventarliste/r1-2-lo3-7-w3-7-maultrommeln/)
- **CLAIMED (as reported).** For Chuvan frame-shaped harps four textural layers are distinguished: "(1) the FF; (2) its subharmonic chest resonance an octave below; (3) the middle melodic layer above FF ...; and (4) a high melodic layer". — [Nikolsky et al. 2020, footnote 62](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.03051/pdf)

### Inferences
- In the kubing spectrum at 70 Hz, 210 Hz is h3 and 770 Hz is h11: neither h1 nor h2 is reported as visible. Together with Leipp and Nikolsky this says that a synthesized drone should not be a strong sine at f0. The drone's loudness is set by the low odd harmonics (h3, h5, h7) and by the line spacing itself (a residue pitch).
- Reasons that follow from section 2 and 3, not stated by a source: a small dipole-like source radiates poorly at 50–100 Hz, and a pulse train puts most of its energy well above the repetition rate.
- Breath is said to strengthen the fundamental (Vandré), so the level of the lowest components is a natural target for a "breath" control.

### Gaps
- No calibrated level of h1 relative to the strongest harmonic was found in the sources read, either for the bare instrument or at the mouth. Vandré's and Leipp's figures show it only graphically.
- The chest "subharmonic" an octave below is reported for one tradition without measurements.

## 5. The pluck: direction, amplitude, onset noises, decay, Q, pitch glide

### Takeaway
Players pluck in both directions and alternate them for fast rhythm. Published numbers for the pluck and the decay are scarce: an encyclopedia citing Leipp 1963 gives a lamella decay of about 20 dB per second; a student measured 0.41–0.72 s for a bamboo kubing; in Leipp's 1967 sonagrams the trace of one stroke lasts about 0.35–0.5 s in the low partials and under 0.2 s above 1.5 kHz (my reading). The decay is longer with a narrower gap, a stiffer hold against the teeth and a lamella resting in the frame plane; no measured Q, tip amplitude or pitch glide of a jaw harp was found.

### Cited Findings

Direction and manner of the pluck
- **DESCRIBED.** "The reed may be plucked by one stretched finger, picking the reed from below and away from the mouth ... The finger may also pick the reed in the opposite direction ... Thus plucked back and forth, melodies with fast rhythm patterns can be played". For a tremolo, three fingers "pick the reed alternately and in quick succession"; this "needs a lot of practice before the plucking gets regular and the reed is prevented from hitting the frame". — [Vandré, Playing techniques](http://www.rvandre.de/playtech.html)
- **CLAIMED (encyclopedias).** The other hand actuates the reed "le plus souvent de l'extérieur vers l'intérieur" [most often from outside inward] — [fr.wikipedia](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guimbarde_(instrument)); "Brnkání je možné oběma směry, zejména při rychlých změnách tónu a tempa hraní" [plucking is possible in both directions, especially for fast playing] — [cs.wikipedia](https://cs.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brumle). The two encyclopedias and Vandré disagree on which direction is the usual one.
- **DESCRIBED.** "L'index de la main droite 'pince' alors l'extrémité de la lame qui 'échappe' et vibre librement plus ou moins longtemps, selon la raideur de la languette et l'amortissement général du système" [the finger plucks the end of the blade, which escapes and vibrates freely for a longer or shorter time, depending on the stiffness of the reed and the overall damping] (p. 9). — [Leipp 1967](https://www.lam.jussieu.fr/Publications/BulletinsGAM/GAM_25-La-Guimbarde.pdf)
- **CLAIMED (maker).** "The pathway of the stroke at the moment when the finger touches the tongue tip should be strictly perpendicular to the frame plane"; an oblique stroke makes the tongue deviate and touch the frame. — [Oberton Pro, handling](https://www.oberton.pro/en/info/handling/)
- **DESCRIBED.** Lamellate (frame-type) harps are plucked at the end of the frame, not on the reed; bamboo types may be struck with the wrist or jerked with a string, in which case "On relâche très rapidement la tension du cordon" [the string tension is released very quickly] so as not to damp the sound (John Wright). — [Vandré](http://www.rvandre.de/buegelrahm.html); [Leipp 1967, pp. 21, 24](https://www.lam.jussieu.fr/Publications/BulletinsGAM/GAM_25-La-Guimbarde.pdf)

Amplitude
- **CLAIMED (maker).** Stroke strength should be limited so that the tip does not "deflect to distances longer than the elbow" (the bent end); as strokes get stronger "loudness is increasing more or less evenly, and then the moment comes when the force is increasing, but loudness is not"; harder strokes crack the reed at its base. Reed stiffness is quoted for a 10 mm tip deflection. — [Oberton Pro FAQ](https://www.oberton.pro/en/info/faq.htm)
- **MEASURED / stated (blown free reeds, for scale).** "it's reasonably common for the tip of the reed tongue to oscillate about its equilibrium position with an amplitude of 15% of the tongue length" — [Cottingham 2011](https://physicstoday.aip.org/features/acoustics-of-free-reed-instruments); reed-organ reeds of 100–200 Hz, tongue about 4 cm: tip amplitude "several millimeters" above a threshold of about 0.2 kPa — [Cottingham, Lilly, Reed](https://www.cs.bu.edu/fac/snyder/TablatureWeb/BERLIN1.PDF); accordion reed f1 (36 mm long): "Bereits im mf stellten wir an der f1-Tonzunge Amplituden > 4 mm fest" — [Ziegenhals 2009](https://www.ifm-zwota.de/tonzun09.pdf)

Onset noises: click, clank, rattle
- **DESCRIBED.** When the crimping is poor or worn, "la lame 'ferraille' sur les bords du cadre" [the blade clatters on the edges of the frame] (p. 8); flexible frames held too tightly bring the arms together "et la lame ferraille" (p. 9); "la languette vient alors frapper les bords des branches en produisant un 'ferraillement' similaire à celui d'une vieille voiture" (p. 24). Klier's criterion of a good harp is that it gives the octave, ninth and tenth of its lowest note "sans bruits parasites" (p. 11). — [Leipp 1967](https://www.lam.jussieu.fr/Publications/BulletinsGAM/GAM_25-La-Guimbarde.pdf)
- **CLAIMED (maker).** A "metallic clank" arises when "the jews harp tongue touches the frame because the player himself presses the frame too hard"; instruments with tiny gaps and soft frames are prone to it. Named causes: squeezing with the hand, pressing too hard on the teeth, squeezing with the lips, moving the lower jaw, an oblique stroke. Dirt in the gaps causes "weak side-sounds – rustle and chatter"; a cracked reed gives a "steep decrease in oscillations duration, and a mild chatter appears, especially at low amplitudes". — [Oberton Pro, handling](https://www.oberton.pro/en/info/handling/); [FAQ](https://www.oberton.pro/en/info/faq.htm)
- **CLAIMED (encyclopedia).** "náraz jazýčku do zubu je bolestivý a může způsobit trvalou frakturu zubu" [the tongue striking a tooth is painful and can fracture it]. — [cs.wikipedia](https://cs.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brumle)
- **MEASURED / CLAIMED (by material, from recordings).** Grass or bark harps: "'Hollow' clicking, very short, dry, yet soft and muffled, clattering at high intensities". Bamboo: "'Hollow' rattling or rasping ... prolonged decay, introducing tremolo at high intensities". Wood: "Clattering, dense and rather dry, with a distinct 'clang' on the onset, which is more pronounced at high intensities ... medium long decay". Bone: "'Hollow' clucking ... well-rolled decay ... quite long-lasting". Metal: "Ringing or buzzing, resonant, very full and rich, the brightest, the most well-defined in pitch ... with the longest decay". — [Nikolsky et al. 2020, Table 2](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.03051/pdf)
- **MEASURED (free reeds, transferable).** A plucked accordion reed starts in both its first and second bending modes, and both generate pulse series in the sound — [Ziegenhals 2009](https://www.ifm-zwota.de/tonzun09.pdf). Blown reeds show the second transverse and first torsional modes in the first stages of the transient — [Cottingham and Wolff 2015](https://viennatalk2015.mdw.ac.at/abstracts/ViennaTalk2015_submission_39.pdf)
- **MEASURED (student).** "The onset duration of each pluck lasts about 0.5 seconds ± 0.1 depending on strength of plucking". The page does not define "onset duration"; the value is of the order of the whole sound of one pluck. — [UBC Phys341, Kumbing](https://wiki.ubc.ca/Course:Phys341_2020/Kumbing)

Decay
- **CLAIMED (encyclopedia citing Leipp 1963).** The reed, a vibrating blade without resonator, has "une décroissance assez rapide, qui peut être de 20 décibels par seconde". — [fr.wikipedia](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guimbarde_(instrument))
- **MEASURED (student; bamboo kubing, tongue 11 cm, 70 Hz).** Decay times by loudness of pluck: "loud (~0.720 sec), soft (~0.406 sec.), medium (~0.569 sec)". "This decay rate does not change drastically with or without the presence of the oral cavity." No threshold or method is stated. — [UBC Phys341, Kumbing](https://wiki.ubc.ca/Course:Phys341_2020/Kumbing)
- **MEASURED (my reading of Figure 6; plucked in front of a microphone; 1-second mark on the time axis).** For each of the three steel harps (about 80, 60, 50 Hz) the trace of a single stroke is visible for roughly 0.35–0.5 s in the partials below about 0.6 kHz and for roughly 0.15–0.2 s above 1.5 kHz; the first 0.05 s or so shows a broadband smear across all partials. The dynamic range of the sonagraph is not stated, so these are durations down to an unknown floor. — [Leipp 1967, p. 12](https://www.lam.jussieu.fr/Publications/BulletinsGAM/GAM_25-La-Guimbarde.pdf)
- **As reported.** Smaller lamella-to-frame distance: spectrum "the longer it is sustained" (Ledang via Morgan); arms too wide: "the sound played would decay much quicker" (Ledang via Li). — [Morgan 2008](https://facultystaff.richmond.edu/~amcgraw/gamelan/ubc_2008_fall_morgan_deirdre-libre.pdf); [Li 2015](https://courses.physics.illinois.edu/phys406/sp2017/Student_Projects/Spring15/Simon_Li_Physics_406_Final_Report_Sp15.pdf)
- **CLAIMED (maker).** With a tongue bent out of the frame plane, "Sound loudness and duration after a single stroke (fade-out time) are decreased"; "the fade out time of the sound is even shorter, as tongue stroke length decreases"; in the extreme, a tongue bent so far that it does not reach the frame plane gives no sound. — [Oberton Pro, nobuzz](https://www.oberton.pro/en/info/nobuzz/)
- **DESCRIBED.** Without the teeth as counter-mass a bow-shaped harp "klingt ... nur ganz kurz" (section 2). — [Vandré](http://www.rvandre.de/buegelrahm.html)
- **MEASURED / INTERPRETATION.** The wooden guimbarde: "le système est plus amorti et l'effet acoustique diffèrent du point de vue du timbre (moins de partiels)" [the system is more damped and the timbre differs: fewer partials] (p. 21). Low-pitched steel harps: "la languette grave est très molle, donc peu 'sonore'" [the low reed is very soft, hence not very sonorous] (p. 17). — [Leipp 1967](https://www.lam.jussieu.fr/Publications/BulletinsGAM/GAM_25-La-Guimbarde.pdf)
- **DESCRIBED (player-researcher).** "The genggong lamella has a relatively short sustain, and must be constantly kept in motion by pulling the attached string." — [Morgan 2008, ch. 5](https://facultystaff.richmond.edu/~amcgraw/gamelan/ubc_2008_fall_morgan_deirdre-libre.pdf)
- **DESCRIBED (deliberate damping).** Staccato: "Avec la langue, je bloque de temps en temps la lame brusquement, comme lorsqu'on prononce 'de de de'" [with my tongue I block the blade abruptly from time to time] (John Wright, p. 23). A thumb touching the tongue "damps the sound". — [Leipp 1967](https://www.lam.jussieu.fr/Publications/BulletinsGAM/GAM_25-La-Guimbarde.pdf); [Oberton Pro, handling](https://www.oberton.pro/en/info/handling/)
- **MODEL (free reeds, for comparison).** "Free reeds in contrast are lightly damped and tend to oscillate close to their natural frequencies." — [Puranik and Scavone 2023](https://dael.euracoustics.org/confs/fa2023/data/articles/000853.pdf)

Pitch during the note
- **MEASURED / INTERPRETATION.** Blowing while plucking: "le son change manifestement de hauteur et de nature ... La baisse du son est normale : la contrainte exercée par le jet d'air sur la lame aboutit au même résultat qu'une surcharge rapportée à celle-ci" [the sound clearly changes in pitch and nature; the lowering is normal: the constraint of the air jet on the blade has the same result as an added load] (p. 16). Asked whether inhaling and exhaling differ: "la hauteur du bourdon ne change pratiquement pas; mais le timbre est différent car la languette est un système dissymétrique" [the pitch of the drone hardly changes, but the timbre differs because the reed is an asymmetric system] (p. 23). — [Leipp 1967](https://www.lam.jussieu.fr/Publications/BulletinsGAM/GAM_25-La-Guimbarde.pdf)
- **DESCRIBED.** After a pluck toward the mouth the finger "knocks several times gently on the lips. At each knock the amplitude and pitch of the jew's harp sound is slightly raised". — [Vandré, Playing techniques](http://www.rvandre.de/playtech.html)
- **MEASURED (blown reed-organ reed, transferable).** "an approximately linear decrease of playing frequency with increasing blowing pressure is normally observed"; in the example figure the frequency axis spans 127–134 Hz for blowing pressures up to about 3.5 kPa (axis values recovered from damaged PDF text). — [Cottingham, Reed, Busha](https://www.cs.bu.edu/fac/snyder/TablatureWeb/BERLIN2.PDF)

### Inferences
- If the lamella's amplitude falls 20 dB per second, its amplitude time constant is 8.69/20 = 0.43 s, T60 is 3 s, and Q = π·f·τ is about 68 at 50 Hz, 96 at 70 Hz, 109 at 80 Hz and 136 at 100 Hz. This is arithmetic on an encyclopedia's paraphrase of a paper I did not read, for one unspecified instrument.
- The audible decay is not the lamella's decay. Because the pulses weaken and widen as the amplitude falls (section 3), the radiated level drops faster than the lamella amplitude, the upper partials first; Leipp's Figure 6 shows the upper partials gone in under 0.2 s while the low ones last about twice as long. A narrow gap keeps the pulses effective at small amplitude, which would explain Ledang's "longer sustained" without any change in the lamella's own damping.
- The onset has, from the sources, up to four ingredients: a short broadband burst (Leipp's sonagram); extra components from the second bending mode and from torsion that fade quickly (Ziegenhals, Cottingham, Leipp); an optional metallic clank of lamella on frame, which is a fault in good playing but characteristic of worn, tightly gripped or organic instruments; and stroke-to-stroke variation of level and spectrum (Leipp). A finger-release click is not described separately by any source.
- Tip speed for scale (mine): 2π·f·A gives 2.5 m/s at 80 Hz and 5 mm, 1.6 m/s at 50 Hz and 5 mm.
- The kubing's decay time grows with pluck strength (0.41, 0.57, 0.72 s). That is what a fixed detection floor does to an exponential decay and need not mean amplitude-dependent damping.

### Gaps
- No measured tip displacement of a plucked jaw harp (mm), no measured attack time in milliseconds, no measured Q or damping ratio, and no decay time of a metal instrument at the mouth, with or without breath, were found. Makers describe "long sustain" without figures.
- No source documents a pitch glide of the unblown lamella during its decay (amplitude-dependent frequency). The only pitch effects reported are those of breath, of touching the reed, and of knocking on the lips.
- Leipp 1963, pp. 393–395, would have to be read to learn what the "20 décibels par seconde" refers to (instrument, conditions, whether lamella velocity or radiated sound).
- The planned searches for these numbers (English, Russian, German) could not be run after the search budget ran out.

## 6. Breath as an energy source; sound levels

### Takeaway
Breath changes the sound in three documented ways: it lowers the pitch slightly, like an added load (Leipp); it makes the sound louder, especially the fundamental (players, encyclopedias, Montagu); and it changes the odd/even balance, differently for inhaling and exhaling because the reed is asymmetric (Leipp). A lamella resting exactly in the frame plane cannot be set going by breath alone; one displaced from it can, and then the instrument works as a free reed, at the cost of loudness and decay time when plucked (maker). No sound level in dB SPL and no measured gain from breath was found.

### Cited Findings
- **MEASURED / INTERPRETATION.** "Lorsqu'on souffle en pinçant la languette, le son change manifestement de hauteur et de nature. En plus de son rôle normal d'excitateur mécanique d'une cavité, la lame fonctionne alors partiellement comme anche libre. ... de lame vibrante encastrée-libre elle devient anche libre." [When one blows while plucking, the sound clearly changes in pitch and nature; besides exciting the cavity mechanically, the blade then works partly as a free reed.] The whole spectrum changes (damaged sentence, see section 3). Skilled players have always used the effect; John Wright said he uses "constamment des aspirations-expirations, mais faibles" [constantly, but weak, inhalations and exhalations] (pp. 16, 18–19). — [Leipp 1967](https://www.lam.jussieu.fr/Publications/BulletinsGAM/GAM_25-La-Guimbarde.pdf)
- **DESCRIBED.** Inhaling against exhaling: same drone pitch, different timbre, "car la languette est un système dissymétrique"; Angus Lawry produces "des aspirations-expirations très brèves" with the diaphragm as a kind of staccato (p. 23). — [Leipp 1967](https://www.lam.jussieu.fr/Publications/BulletinsGAM/GAM_25-La-Guimbarde.pdf)
- **DESCRIBED.** "When breathing in and out, the air stream amplifies the fundamental note"; breathing between plucks produces "rasping sounds". — [Vandré, Playing techniques](http://www.rvandre.de/playtech.html)
- **CLAIMED.** "Most good players blow on the reed as well as plucking it, which both amplifies and enriches the sound". — [Montagu](https://www.jeremymontagu.co.uk/Trumps%20(Jews%20Harps)%20-%20Jeremy%20Montagu.epub). "Durch das gleichzeitige Hindurchblasen bzw. -ziehen von Luft wird dieser Klang verstärkt." [Blowing or drawing air through at the same time strengthens the sound.] — [de.wikipedia](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maultrommel)
- **MEASURED (student).** "Breathing in and out acts like a driven force, enhancing the force of the airflow. It should not change the general spectrum except for the amplitude of some harmonics"; the measured spectra while breathing out kept their general shape. — [Li 2015](https://courses.physics.illinois.edu/phys406/sp2017/Student_Projects/Spring15/Simon_Li_Physics_406_Final_Report_Sp15.pdf)
- **MEASURED (student).** With breathing, the kubing's spectrum gains amplitude "along a wide range of the lower frequencies from 280 Hz to 1 kHz". — [UBC Phys341, Kumbing](https://wiki.ubc.ca/Course:Phys341_2020/Kumbing)
- **DESCRIBED.** Self-sounding: jew's harps "begin to sound without being plucked, just by breathing in or out" if "the reed has to be shifted a little from the level of the frame, and the air flow has to pull it back towards the frame level" (explanation credited to Clemens Voigt of Dan Moi); on bow-shaped harps the thumb pushes the reed base to shift it. "In this way the jew's harp works just like a harmonica." — [Vandré, Tones and Pitches](http://www.rvandre.de/tones.html)
- **CLAIMED (maker).** "Buzzing is enabled by one certain feature of the instrument structure: its tongue must be bent away from the frame plane. If it is located strictly parallel to the frame, right between the decks edges, one cannot agitate it even by strongest breath." A permanently bent tongue is called a defect that lowers loudness and fade-out time and hides "gentle overtones"; a normal harp can be made to buzz by pressing on the tongue to bend it temporarily. — [Oberton Pro, nobuzz](https://www.oberton.pro/en/info/nobuzz/)
- **CLAIMED (encyclopedia).** "Dobrý nástroj zazní svým základním tónem i bez brnknutí při pouhém prosátí vzduchu štěrbinou, podobně jako jazýček harmonikového 'hlasu'." [A good instrument sounds its fundamental without a pluck, merely by drawing air through the slot, like a harmonica reed.] — [cs.wikipedia](https://cs.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brumle). This conflicts with the maker's view that a harp that buzzes is a worse harp.
- **CLAIMED.** "Some jew's harps can be activated by the breath, even if they are made for plucking"; footnote: "This quality is also found in some heteroglot jew's harps with particularly thin and flexible lamellae." The Balinese enggung is a pure breath-driven free reed of jaw harp form. — [Kolltveit 2016](https://musark.no/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/J-Harps-of-Bone-Wood-and-Metal.Kolltveit-2016.pdf)
- **MEASURED (free reeds, transferable).** "at low air flow rates aerodynamic forces add to the mechanical damping of the vibrating reed, but at higher flow rates, the aerodynamic forces contribute negative damping resulting in self-sustained oscillations"; maximum growth "around 1.0-1.5 kPa" (restored by me from OCR-damaged text, "1.0-I .5 kPa"); threshold for reed-organ reeds "about 0.2 kPa". — [Cottingham, Reed, Busha](https://www.cs.bu.edu/fac/snyder/TablatureWeb/BERLIN2.PDF); [Cottingham, Lilly, Reed](https://www.cs.bu.edu/fac/snyder/TablatureWeb/BERLIN1.PDF)
- **MODEL (free reeds).** The rest offset of the reed above its plate ("Aufbiegung", 0.5 mm on a 36 mm accordion reed) "ist wichtig für das Entstehen der Selbsterregung" [is important for self-excitation to arise]. — [Ziegenhals 2009](https://www.ifm-zwota.de/tonzun09.pdf)
- **MEASURED / stated (free reeds).** Asian free reeds, cut in one piece with their frame, rest in the plane of the frame: "The reed tongue is positioned so that absent any pressure difference, it is in the closed position ... In many instruments, including the khaen ..., a single reed functions with both directions of airflow. Because of their structure, the Eastern reeds must generally be coupled to a resonator". — [Cottingham 2011](https://physicstoday.aip.org/features/acoustics-of-free-reed-instruments)
- **Sound level, qualitative only.** The instrument's decline is "probablement liée à son faible niveau" [probably linked to its low level] (p. 9); asked about its range outdoors: "malgré un niveau faible il peut se transmettre à une assez grande distance sans être détruit par le bruit de fond. Mais ce problème reste à étudier." [despite a low level it can carry quite far without being drowned by background noise; but this remains to be studied] (p. 24). — [Leipp 1967](https://www.lam.jussieu.fr/Publications/BulletinsGAM/GAM_25-La-Guimbarde.pdf)
- **CLAIMED.** Metal harps are "harmonically richer and louder than JHs made from organic materials, especially in high range". — [Nikolsky et al. 2020, Table 3](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.03051/pdf)

### Inferences
- The free-reed measurements suggest that weak breath through a centred lamella may add damping, not gain (Cottingham: at low flow the aerodynamic forces add damping). The sources that report amplification are therefore better read as: breath adds a steady flow that the vibrating lamella chops, which raises the radiated level, the fundamental most of all, without necessarily feeding the lamella's vibration. Sustaining or amplifying the vibration itself needs an offset rest position and enough pressure, or, for a reed resting in the frame plane as in the khaen, a resonator that the reed can couple to. None of the jaw harp sources separates these two effects, and none says whether the mouth cavity can act as such a resonator for a jaw harp lamella.
- The pitch drop under breath is small in the free-reed data (a few hertz in 130 over 1–3 kPa, which is far above gentle breath), consistent with Leipp's "ne change pratiquement pas" for in against out.
- Asymmetry: with an inhale the steady flow passes the slot in the opposite direction, so the larger of the two pulses per cycle swaps; a timbre difference between in and out follows directly from the two-pulse picture of section 3.

### Gaps
- No measured sound pressure level of a jaw harp (dB SPL at a stated distance) was found, and no measured level difference between playing with and without breath, or between exhaling and inhaling.
- No measurement of mouth pressure or flow during jaw harp playing was found.
- The one clean statement about how breath changes the harmonics (Leipp, p. 16) is damaged in the scan and inconsistent as printed.

## 7. Idioglot (bamboo, palm wood, bone, brass leaf) versus heteroglot (forged frame with steel lamella)

### Takeaway
Reported differences are qualitative. Organic idioglot instruments are more damped, radiate fewer partials, deviate more from the harmonic series and carry clicks, rattles and an onset "clang"; metal instruments have the longest decay, the brightest and most homogeneous tone, and are louder, especially in the high range. Thin brass idioglot harps (đàn môi, kouxian) are a different case: their very small gap gives a full, stable spectrum, they are plucked at the frame and need no teeth.

### Cited Findings
- **MEASURED / INTERPRETATION.** For the wooden (bamboo) guimbarde the mechanism "reste identique", but "le système est plus amorti et l'effet acoustique diffèrent du point de vue du timbre (moins de partiels)". Its dimensions are set so that, when the end of the instrument is plucked, the tongue vibrates "plus ou moins en opposition de phase" with the body (p. 21). — [Leipp 1967](https://www.lam.jussieu.fr/Publications/BulletinsGAM/GAM_25-La-Guimbarde.pdf)
- **MEASURED / CLAIMED (from recordings of five materials).** Sound qualities by material are quoted in section 5 (grass, bamboo, wood, bone, metal). In addition: organic framed harps give a "Loud, low, dry, and harsh sound that lacks in homogeneity ('dirty') due to either rattling, rasping or bifurcated 'clang'"; metal gives a "Prolonged ringing or buzzing sound, harmonically richer and louder than JHs made from organic materials, especially in high range". "JHs made of organic materials usually deviate from the harmonic series in the tuning of partials." Hybrid harps with a bamboo frame and a metal lamella do "not substantially differ in spectral texture from the frame-shaped instruments that are made just out of bamboo". — [Nikolsky et al. 2020, Tables 2 and 3, footnote 65](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.03051/pdf)
- **CLAIMED.** "Бамбуковые рамочные варганы дребезжат, даже «тремолируют» при высокой интенсивности звукоизвлечения" [bamboo frame harps rattle, even "tremolo", at high playing intensity]; frame harps have a pronounced pedal tone in the bass. — [Nikolsky et al. 2021 (Russian)](https://lang-folk.ru/sites/default/files/2026-09/2021_9_31_Nikolsky.pdf)
- **CLAIMED.** A standard size for frame-shaped instruments of the Sarmatian period in Central Asia and southern Siberia: 10.3–12.5 × 1.4–1.7 × 0.1–0.2 cm (after Borodovskii 2017). — [Nikolsky et al. 2020](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.03051/pdf)
- **CLAIMED (as reported).** Picken: East Asian bamboo instruments are made "with an extremely precise adjustment of the lamella", and "in their neglect of this refinement, the iron and steel jew's harps of Central, South and West Asia, and of Europe as well, must be regarded as degenerate"; he argues for "regarding the finely wrought bamboo jew's harps of East Asia as interruptive aerophones in a class of their own". — [Kolltveit 2016](https://musark.no/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/J-Harps-of-Bone-Wood-and-Metal.Kolltveit-2016.pdf); [Kolltveit 2006](https://musark.no/wp-content/uploads/2013/11/Thesis-intro.pdf)
- **CLAIMED (museum).** Đàn môi: one piece of brass about 0.5 mm thick, tongue cut to a point, played between the lips; "Aufgrund des minimalen Abstands von Zunge zu Rahmen weist die Đàn môi ein sehr volles, stabiles Spektrum auf". Kouxian leaves are about 0.2 mm brass. — [Universität Würzburg](https://www.musikwissenschaft.uni-wuerzburg.de/musikinstrumente/bestand/inventarliste/r1-2-lo3-7-w3-7-maultrommeln/)
- **DESCRIBED.** Frame (lamellate) harps are held at the lips only and "klingen dennoch genauso voll und kräftig" [still sound just as full and strong] as bow harps held against the teeth. — [Vandré](http://www.rvandre.de/buegelrahm.html)
- **CLAIMED (vendor table).** Two đàn môi in the table: "Hoa Binh", gaps 85 µm, 53 Hz; "Da", gaps 20 µm, 113 Hz. — [Oberton Pro table](https://www.oberton.pro/en/lab.htm?sort=frequency_avg)
- **MEASURED (student).** Bamboo kubing: 70 Hz, decay 0.41–0.72 s (section 5). — [UBC Phys341, Kumbing](https://wiki.ubc.ca/Course:Phys341_2020/Kumbing)
- **DESCRIBED / as reported.** The Balinese genggong is an idioglot harp "made from the leaf stem of the jaka sugar palm tree", "from 14cm to over 20cm" long and "from about 1.6cm to 2cm" wide, set in motion by jerking a string; the lamella alone gives "complex non-harmonic partials" (Rai 2004); it has "a relatively short sustain"; players choose pairs by the overtones, not the fundamental, and play with an open glottis. — [Morgan 2008, chs. 3 and 5](https://facultystaff.richmond.edu/~amcgraw/gamelan/ubc_2008_fall_morgan_deirdre-libre.pdf)
- **CLAIMED.** Idioglot bamboo forms are the ones for which the free-reed comparison is closest: mouth-organ reeds "are often like miniature idioglot jew's harps", and some can be sounded by breath. — [Kolltveit 2016](https://musark.no/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/J-Harps-of-Bone-Wood-and-Metal.Kolltveit-2016.pdf)

### Inferences
- The split that matters acoustically is not idioglot against heteroglot but material and gap: bamboo, wood and bone have high internal damping (short decay, fewer partials) and rattle at high amplitude; thin brass idioglot harps and steel heteroglot harps both ring, and a tight gap gives either a rich spectrum.
- For a synthesis preset, "organic" would mean: shorter decay (a few tenths of a second), fewer and less exactly harmonic partials, an onset clang and amplitude-dependent rattle; "metal" would mean: longest decay, lines up to several kilohertz, clean harmonicity.

### Gaps
- No side-by-side measurement (same microphone, same player) of an idioglot and a heteroglot instrument with numbers for decay, number of partials or level was found. Nikolsky's comparison is of field recordings, described in words.
- Nothing quantitative was found on mukkuri, morsing or kubing beyond the student page; the search for Japanese and Indian studies could not be run.

## 8. Transferable physics: blown free reeds, plucked tines, and the jaw harp as a plucked free reed

### Takeaway
Free-reed research supplies what the jaw harp literature lacks: the reed moves almost sinusoidally in its first cantilever mode while the pressure is pulse-like, because the reed is a valve that closes the slot twice per cycle; the radiated sound of a blown reed is a dipole from the flow through the gaps; a plucked reed in its plate radiates pulse series locked to each excited mode; and self-oscillation needs flow, an offset and upstream air inertia. Tines (kalimba) show how strongly a mid-span support or profile shifts the inharmonic mode ratios. The only paper that treats the jaw harp explicitly as a pulse source driving the mouth cavity is Adkins 1974.

### Cited Findings
- **MEASURED.** Blown reed-organ reeds: nearly sinusoidal reed motion at normal pressure, in contrast with the pulse-like pressure near the reed; the reed opens and closes the frame opening "twice in each cycle"; displacement profile fits the first cantilever mode; second beam mode appears only above normal playing pressure; the reed's equilibrium shifts "about 1-2 mm in the direction of airflow". — [Cottingham, Lilly, Reed](https://www.cs.bu.edu/fac/snyder/TablatureWeb/BERLIN1.PDF)
- **MEASURED / MODEL.** "the sounding frequency of the blown reed is somewhat below the natural vibrating frequency of the reed tongue"; two unequal puffs per cycle; Western reeds sound with one direction of airflow only, Asian free reeds are cut from one piece with the frame and a single reed works with both directions. — [Cottingham 2011](https://physicstoday.aip.org/features/acoustics-of-free-reed-instruments)
- **MODEL (abstract only).** For the accordion reed, self-sustained oscillation occurs "without acoustic coupling"; "The excitation force is found to be associated with the inertial load of the unsteady flow through the reed gaps"; far-field sound "dominated by the dipolar source associated by the strong momentum fluctuations of the flow through the reed gaps". — [Ricot, Caussé, Misdariis 2005](https://doi.org/10.1121/1.1852546)
- **MODEL.** St. Hilaire's mechanism as summarized: the Bernoulli mechanism "in isolation cannot develop the asymmetric pressure differences required to set up self-oscillations"; the inertia of the upstream air gives "a component of the pressure near the reed changing in phase with the reed velocity, thus leading to a net positive power transfer to the reed". — [Puranik and Scavone 2023](https://dael.euracoustics.org/confs/fa2023/data/articles/000853.pdf)
- **MODEL (abstract only).** A two-parameter nonlinear theory (jet contraction and aerodynamic damping) accounts for threshold pressure, large-signal amplitude and frequency variation of blown reed valves. — [Tarnopolsky, Fletcher, Lai 2000](https://doi.org/10.1121/1.429473)
- **MODEL (a simple valve-area model with numbers).** Reed plate of an accordion f1 reed: slot length l = 36 mm, width b = 4 mm, plate thickness d = 3 mm, rest offset a = 0.5 mm. The opening is taken as F = (l + b)(a + h·sin ωt) while the reed is above the plate, zero while it is inside the slot, and opens again below the plate when a + h·sin ωt < −d; the flow saturates when the tip amplitude h exceeds 3.1 mm, and the reed swings out below the plate above the same 3.1 mm. "Der durch den wechselnden Fluss entstehende Schalldruck ist proportional zur Ableitung des Volumenstromes" [the sound pressure is proportional to the time derivative of the volume flow]. The author states that the model matches the measured pressure only "in sehr grober Näherung", mainly because the real reed bends and is not a rigid flap. — [Ziegenhals 2009](https://www.ifm-zwota.de/tonzun09.pdf)
- **MEASURED.** Harmonium reed B5 (990 Hz; model dimensions 16 × 2 × 0.4 mm): the reed velocity spectrum shows stronger peaks near the fundamental and near the third natural frequency (about 16,000 Hz), but "a clear formant is not visible at the second natural frequency (≈ 6000Hz)"; torsional modes are named as a possible cause. — [Puranik and Scavone 2023](https://dael.euracoustics.org/confs/fa2023/data/articles/000853.pdf)
- **MEASURED / stated.** "a plucked reed will oscillate at a natural frequency determined by its mass distribution and stiffness"; the blown harmonica reed sounds near that plucked frequency, shifted by coupling to the player's airway. — [Bahnson, Antaki, Beery 1998](https://www.cs.bu.edu/fac/snyder/TablatureWeb/Bahnson%20JASA%201998.pdf)
- **MEASURED (abstract only; plucked tines).** Kalimba: the first three partials of each tine "are identified as the first three modes of transverse vibration of a beam"; "the overtone sequence is inharmonic"; with the tine clamped at one end, supported at the bridge and free at the tip, "the clamped-supported-free frequency ratios f2/f1 and f3/f1 are not fixed values, but depend uniquely upon where the bridge supports and subdivides the tine". — [Chapman 2012](https://doi.org/10.1121/1.3651090)
- **MODEL (abstract only; the jaw harp as a pulse source coupled to the mouth).** Low mouth tunings: the cavity's ring time exceeds the pulse period and "the harmonic series of the fundamental frequency of the lamella dominates the sound". High tunings: "the mouth cavity and ear respond to each successive pulse independently", so the heard pitch follows the mouth resonance and "may be tuned continuously". — [Adkins 1974](https://doi.org/10.1121/1.1914580)
- **MODEL.** Leipp makes the same low/high distinction from damping: with an 80 Hz harp only 18 partials lie between 500 and 2000 Hz and the scale is discrete; with a 50 Hz harp there are 30, the first usable pair is only "40 savarts ... (160 cents, ou trois quarts de ton)" apart, "les raies spectrales s'élargissent et finissent par se toucher : on peut alors obtenir des sons de hauteur continuement variable" [the spectral lines widen and end up touching: one can then obtain sounds of continuously variable pitch] (p. 17). — [Leipp 1967](https://www.lam.jussieu.fr/Publications/BulletinsGAM/GAM_25-La-Guimbarde.pdf)

### Inferences
- The jaw harp differs from a blown free reed in three ways that matter for a model: energy comes from the pluck and only decays; the lamella rests in the frame plane (offset near zero), so the two pulses per cycle are nearly symmetric and of opposite sign, which gives odd harmonics, where a blown reed gives two same-sign unequal puffs; and the "resonator" is the vocal tract, strongly damped and tuned far above the reed, so it filters the pulses and does not pull the reed's frequency.
- Ziegenhals's area function is a usable starting point for a source model of the plucked case: make the gap flow, or directly the pressure pulse, a function of the lamella's position relative to the frame plane and of its velocity, drive it with a decaying sinusoid at f0 (plus a quickly decaying second-mode term for the attack), and differentiate. His own caveat applies: a rigid-flap model reproduces measured waveforms only roughly.
- A pulse-excited resonator reproduces Adkins's two regimes without special handling: a lightly damped low formant integrates over several pulses (harmonics of f0 stand out), a high or heavily damped one rings once per pulse (a formant pitch that glides continuously).
- Kalimba tines are not a close model for the lamella's partials: there the bar's own inharmonic modes radiate through a soundboard, here the slot pulses do. They are relevant only for the brief modal content of the attack.

### Gaps
- No paper was found that models or synthesizes the jaw harp as a plucked free reed coupled to a vocal tract (physical-modelling, DAFx, ISMA or JASA); the search for it returned nothing before the budget ran out, which is weak evidence of absence.
- Trias 2010 (Telemark) builds "an acoustic model of the instrument" from the literature, according to the author's summary, but the thesis itself was not reachable.
- Fletcher and Rossing's book, Millot and Baumann 2007, St. Hilaire 1971 and work on music-box combs were not consulted; nothing is cited from them directly.
- The venue of the two Cottingham proceedings papers hosted at cs.bu.edu is my assumption from the file names.
